What is actually being priced

  • At ~$0.60B EV, the market is paying for a probability-weighted U.S. profit share in a niche EGFR segment, plus something for CLN-978 — and very little for CLN-049 or velinotamig.
  • Zipalertinib's dataset is not best-in-class on response rate (35% overall vs sunvozertinib's labeled 46%, different populations) — its edge is post-amivantamab experience, oral dosing, and a positive randomized Phase 3 in first-line.

  • Accelerated approval needs a confirmatory story. No dedicated confirmatory trial in the monotherapy population has been publicly designated; REZILIENT3 is a first-line chemotherapy-combination trial, not a monotherapy confirmation (§05).
  • CLN-978's data are n-of-few. Remissions in refractory SLE/RA are striking, but cohorts are single-digit to ~29 patients, uncontrolled, with follow-up measured in weeks (§04).
  • Burn vs. runway. The company guides runway "into 2029"; H1 2026 cash use annualizes to a shorter runway unless spend moderates or milestones land (§10).

03 / SCIENCE

Three mechanisms, three different failure modes

Zipalertinib is a chemistry answer to a structural problem. CLN-978 and CLN-049 are T-cell engagers whose risk is not binding but biology — whether depleting a lineage produces durable remission without unacceptable immune toxicity. Velinotamig extends the same logic one step deeper, to the plasma cells that antibodies and CD19-directed agents cannot reach.

Zipalertinib — plain English

Some lung cancers are driven by a mutated growth switch (EGFR). In the exon-20-insertion variant, a few extra amino acids are wedged into the switch right next to where drugs need to sit. The wedge both locks the switch "on" and reshapes the pocket so that standard EGFR drugs either can't bind tightly or can't avoid also hitting the normal, healthy version of the switch found in skin and gut — which is why first-generation attempts produced rash and diarrhea before they produced tumor shrinkage. Zipalertinib is a small oral molecule, dosed twice daily, shaped to fit the wedged pocket and largely ignore the normal switch.

Zipalertinib — technical

FACT Exon 20 insertions (most commonly at residues ~762–774, the loop following the αC-helix) shift the αC-helix and P-loop into the drug-binding pocket, producing an active kinase conformation whose pocket sterically resembles wild-type EGFR — the structural reason non-selective TKIs fail on therapeutic index (Robichaux et al., Nature 2018; Vyse & Huang, reviews of ex20ins structural biology). Zipalertinib (CLN-081/TAS6417, originated by Taiho) is an irreversible, covalent EGFR inhibitor (Cys797-binding) selected for potency against ex20ins variants with relative sparing of wild-type EGFR; it is dosed 100 mg twice daily in the pivotal program.

INTERPRETATION WT-sparing is relative, not absolute — the label-relevant evidence is the adverse-event table, not the binding assay. In the REZILIENT1 Phase 2b safety population (n=244), paronychia (38.5%), rash (30.3%), dermatitis acneiform, dry skin, diarrhea (21.7%) and stomatitis (20.1%) were the most common treatment-related events — an unmistakably EGFR-class profile, mostly grade 1–2, with grade ≥3 pneumonitis at 2.5% the signal that matters most. The molecule is cleaner than pan-EGFR inhibition; it is not clean.

FACT CNS: data, but thin data. Zipalertinib showed systemic responses in patients with baseline brain metastases in REZILIENT1 (ORR 31%, n=68) and a PFS hazard ratio of 0.38 in the brain-metastasis subgroup of REZILIENT3; in REZILIENT2's small CNS cohort (n=16), intracranial ORR was 31.3% with median intracranial duration of response of 8.1 months. INTERPRETATION That is consistent with CNS penetration, but no pivotal cohort was powered on intracranial endpoints, and "CNS-active" claims beyond these numbers are UNVERIFIED marketing extrapolation.

INTERPRETATION Why it might fail: (1) response rate — 35% overall in the filing population is mid-pack for the class, and cross-trial comparisons against sunvozertinib's 46% will be made by every reviewer and payer regardless of population differences; (2) resistance — covalent Cys797 inhibitors invite C797S and bypass-track resistance, untested at scale here; (3) the confirmatory-evidence problem in §05; (4) pneumonitis/ILD is a class warning on the competitor's label and a grade ≥3 signal already present in zipalertinib's own data.

CLN-978 — CD19×CD3 T-cell engager

FACT CLN-978 is a 65 kDa bispecific T-cell engager: a very-high-affinity anti-CD19 scFv, an anti-CD3 scFv, and an albumin-binding single-domain antibody for half-life extension. It is subcutaneously administered, wholly owned, and dosed in micrograms (single target doses of 10–45 µg in Phase 1). The design thesis is that very high CD19 affinity permits depletion of B cells — including low-CD19 plasmablasts and tissue-resident B cells that monoclonal antibodies spare — at doses low enough to keep cytokine release manageable in an outpatient setting. INTERPRETATION The "immune reset" claim (deplete, let a naïve repertoire reconstitute, durable treatment-free remission) is borrowed from the academic CD19 CAR-T experience in SLE; whether a transient engager reproduces it is exactly what Phase 1/2 must prove, and 8-week follow-up cannot.

CLN-049 — FLT3×CD3 T-cell engager

FACT CLN-049 binds the extracellular domain of FLT3 — wild-type and mutated — which is expressed on AML blasts in >80% of patients, and redirects T cells against them regardless of FLT3 mutational status. It is licensed exclusively, worldwide, from DKFZ/Tübingen. The mechanistic risk is on-target: FLT3 is also expressed on normal hematopoietic progenitors, so the therapeutic window rests on expression density and dose titration (the program uses step-up dosing).

Velinotamig — BCMA×CD3 T-cell engager

FACT Velinotamig targets BCMA on long-lived plasma cells — the compartment that keeps secreting autoantibodies after CD19-directed B-cell depletion. Cullinan holds an exclusive license for all fields ex-Greater China from Genrix Bio (terms in §07/§10). The scientific logic is complementary to CLN-978; the evidentiary base today is a Phase 1 in China run by the licensor, with the first detailed autoimmune data in two patients (§04).

04 / CLINICAL

What the trials actually showed

Zipalertinib carries the only mature dataset: a single-arm pivotal study behind the pending NDA and a positive randomized Phase 3 behind the next one. Everything else is Phase 1 — reported here with the denominators attached, because in the T-cell-engager programs the denominators are the story.

REZILIENT1 Phase 2b — the NDA dataset (NCT04036682)

FACT Open-label Phase 1/2; the Phase 2b primary efficacy population (N=176) received zipalertinib 100 mg twice daily after prior platinum-based chemotherapy, with or without prior amivantamab. Co-primary endpoints: confirmed ORR and duration of response by blinded independent central review (RECIST v1.1). Median follow-up 9.3 months at the ASCO 2025 primary presentation (Yu et al.); a peer-reviewed report of the study has since appeared in the Journal of Clinical Oncology.

REZILIENT2 Phase 2 (NCT05967689) — breadth cohorts

FACT Recruiting parallel-cohort Phase 2 (Taiho; registry estimate 220 patients) in ex20ins and uncommon non-ex20ins EGFR mutations. Reported cohorts (WCLC/ESMO 2025): uncommon non-ex20ins mutations (n=40; March 2025 cutoff) — confirmed ORR 30%, median DOR 7.75 months; CNS-metastasis subset (n=16; February 2025 cutoff) — intracranial ORR 31.3%, intracranial disease control 68.8%, median intracranial DOR 8.1 months. INTERPRETATION Optionality, not value driver: these cohorts support label-breadth ambitions and the CNS narrative, at Taiho's pace.

CLN-978 Phase 1 — OUTRACE SLE (NCT06613360), OUTRACE RA (NCT06994143), Sjögren's (NCT07041099)

FACT Global Phase 1 single- and multi-dose studies in treatment-refractory disease; background immunosuppressants were discontinued before dosing. EULAR 2026 dataset (29 patients evaluated: SLE n=18, RA n=11; May 2026 cutoffs): in SLE, 10 of 14 patients with ≥4 weeks' follow-up had a ≥4-point hSLEDAI reduction after a single target dose and 5 achieved DORIS remission; lab markers (anti-dsDNA, UPCR, C3/C4) improved where abnormal at baseline; lupus-nephritis patients showed rapid proteinuria improvement. In RA, multi-dose data included a DAS28-ESR remission (4.0 → 2.2 at week 4, maintained at week 8) in a patient refractory to rituximab, with autoantibody reductions. Peripheral B-cell depletion was deep and dose-dependent, with clearance of CD19+ cells documented in lymph-node and synovial tissue. Safety: most CRS was grade 1 and followed the first (10 µg) dose; one grade 3 CRS occurred at the 45 µg target dose and that cohort was discontinued; no ICANS was observed. INTERPRETATION For a Phase 1 of a T-cell engager in autoimmune disease, this is an unusually coherent package — depletion depth, tissue clearance, clinical scores moving together. It is also uncontrolled, unblinded, and weeks deep. Regression to the mean and background-therapy washout effects cannot be excluded; the December 2026 multi-dose readouts (§08) are the next real test.

Velinotamig Phase 1 (Genrix Bio, China) — SLE

FACT In the first two refractory SLE patients completing four IV doses (3 µg/kg, then 10 µg/kg ×3), SLEDAI-2K fell from 16 → 0 and 14 → 2 by week 8, both achieved complete renal response, and no CRS or ICANS was observed (data cutoff May 15, 2026; Cullinan Immunology Day). INTERPRETATION Two patients is a signal, not a dataset — flagged as such. Multi-dose data are slated for ACR Convergence on Nov 8, 2026.

CLN-049 Phase 1 — R/R AML/MDS (NCT05143996)

FACT First-in-human dose escalation (ASH 2025 oral; August 2025 cutoff): 45 patients (39 AML, 3 MDS/AML, 3 MDS), median 2 prior therapies, target doses 1.5–12 µg/kg IV. At the highest target dose (12 µg/kg; n=16): CR/CRh rate 31% (5/16) and composite CR rate 31%. At target doses ≥6 µg/kg (n=32): CR/CRh 25% (8/32), CRc 28% (9/32); among TP53-mutated patients at 12 µg/kg, 4 of 8 achieved CR/CRh; MRD negativity was observed in a subset of responders, and 2 responders proceeded to transplant. Safety: CRS 35.6% (nearly all grade 1–2; no grade 3 CRS with two step-up doses; no CRS discontinuations), infusion reactions 33.3%, febrile neutropenia 20.0%. Responses occurred independent of baseline FLT3 expression. INTERPRETATION A ~30% CR/CRh rate in unselected, heavily pretreated R/R AML — including TP53-mutated disease — from a monotherapy engager is a strong Phase 1 signal by AML standards. The registrational bet (§05) is that a single-arm Phase 2 at the chosen dose reproduces it in a larger, defined population.

05 / REGULATORY

One accelerated approval, one Phase-3-based filing, and an unanswered confirmatory question

Zipalertinib's monotherapy filing is an accelerated-approval application resting on response rate. Under current FDA practice, accelerated approval requires a confirmatory trial underway — and the sponsors have not publicly said which trial that is. That silence is the single largest regulatory risk in this dossier.

12 / RISKS

Ranked red flags

Ranked by expected damage to equity value, not by likelihood alone. The top two are structural — they persist even if every trial works.

  1. The confirmatory-trial gap (zipalertinib monotherapy). GAP No publicly designated confirmatory trial exists for the accelerated-approval population, REZILIENT3 does not map onto it, and FDA has already issued a CRL to a competitor in this exact segment partly on confirmatory readiness (poziotinib, 2022) and overseen a withdrawal after a failed confirmatory trial (mobocertinib, 2023). This is the dossier's #1 red flag because it is binary, dated (Feb 27, 2027), and entirely outside investors' visibility.
  2. Partner dependence on the lead asset. FACT Taiho controls zipalertinib's development, regulatory interactions and commercialization; Cullinan receives 50% of U.S. pre-tax profit and cannot set price, pace, or label strategy. Taiho's independent-indication development costs are 50%-deductible from Cullinan's future profit pool. A disagreement, a reprioritization inside Taiho, or a conservative label negotiation all land directly on CGEM's largest asset with no Cullinan recourse visible in the filed agreement summary.
  3. Competitive compression. FACT Sunvozertinib is approved, oral, and ~19 months ahead with a higher headline ORR (46% vs 35%, different populations); amivantamab-based regimens are entrenched in first-line and expanding (including subcutaneous delivery). Zipalertinib's defensible niches — post-amivantamab, first-line-with-chemo — are real but narrower than "EGFR ex20ins" as a headline.
  4. Monotherapy relevance decay. INTERPRETATION If the first-line combination and amivantamab regimens absorb patients earlier, the post-platinum monotherapy population — the February filing — shrinks and skews more refractory (where zipalertinib's ORR was 24–27%, §04). Approval and commercial significance are different events.
  5. CLN-978 evidentiary thinness. FACT The autoimmune thesis rests on ≤29-patient, uncontrolled cohorts with weeks of follow-up, and the dose ceiling has already appeared once (grade 3 CRS at 45 µg ended that cohort). Autoimmune T-cell engagers are a crowded, well-capitalized race; a single serious infection or a durability failure in the December multi-dose data re-rates the program from "platform" to "project."
  6. CLN-049 single-arm registrational risk. INTERPRETATION A seamless dose-optimization → single-arm expansion design asks FDA to accept ~30% CR/CRh as an accelerated-approval basis in R/R AML. Dose selection error, FLT3 on-target hematologic toxicity at higher exposure, or simple regression in a larger population each independently break the plan; the Fast Track designation de-risks communication, not outcome.
  7. Burn vs. guided runway. FACT H1 2026 operating cash use of $84.3M annualizes well short of the company's into-2029 runway framing, with two Phase 2 programs still ramping. A raise before the autoimmune data mature (2027–28) is the base expectation in this dossier's financial read, at whatever price the February decision leaves behind.
  8. Licensed-asset stack. FACT Three of the four ranked programs are licensed (Taiho, DKFZ/Tübingen, Genrix), carrying up to $692M of contingent Genrix payments alone plus royalties. Success is taxed at every layer; only CLN-978 is wholly owned — which is precisely why its December data carry disproportionate valuation weight.
13 / OPEN

What this dossier could not establish

Open items are listed rather than smoothed over. Each is a specific, answerable question with a named venue where the answer should appear.

  • GAP Confirmatory plan for the monotherapy accelerated approval — which study, which population, agreed with FDA or not. Venue: the approval letter/PMR list (Feb 2027) or a CRL. Until then this is unresolvable from public sources.
  • GAP Identity of Taiho's independent Phase 3 indication (initiated Nov 2025 under the co-development agreement) — unnamed in the FY2025 10-K; watch Taiho's registry postings and Cullinan's Q3 10-Q.
  • GAP REZILIENT3 tolerability detail — discontinuation rates and dose modifications for the combination were not in the WCLC releases; the full publication will settle whether 87% grade ≥3 AEs came with manageable attrition.
  • GAP Q3 2026 financials — burn with both Phase 2 programs running; 10-Q expected November 2026 (inferred from cadence).
  • GAP CLN-978 durability — no data beyond ~8–12 weeks on remission persistence after dosing stops, and no infection-rate picture under sustained deep B-cell depletion. December 2026 multi-dose data address the first, not the second.
  • GAP Ex-U.S. zipalertinib economics — no royalty or profit share to Cullinan outside the U.S. is disclosed in the FY2025 10-K; whether "undisclosed" means "none" is not stated in so many words.
  • GAP First-line NDA review terms — priority review, advisory committee, and PDUFA for the combination filing will only exist after submission completion (expected YE 2026) and FDA acceptance.
  • GAP CLN-049 recommended Phase 2 dose — final selection from the 1.5–12 µg/kg escalation, and the Phase 2's exact eligibility/MRD framework, pending the December 2026 update.
