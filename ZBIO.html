01 / FRAME

A positive Phase 3 that the market graded against someone else's trial

INDIGO did what it was designed to do: obexelimab cut IgG4-RD flare risk 56% versus placebo with all four key secondaries significant and a cleaner safety ledger than placebo. The stock fell anyway, because MITIGATE—inebilizumab's trial—cut flare risk 87%. The investable question is not whether 0.44 is worse than 0.13 on paper (it is). It is whether the difference survives contact with the two trials' designs, and whether a weekly at-home injection can take share from a twice-yearly infusion even with a smaller hazard ratio.

Why the post-INDIGO price may over-discount

  • INDIGO is the largest IgG4-RD trial run (N=194) and is NEJM-published with an accompanying editorial; the primary endpoint was adjudicated, prespecified, and met at p=0.0005—not a marginal hit.
  • The placebo arms of INDIGO and MITIGATE behaved almost identically (annualized flare rate 0.70 vs 0.71; flare incidence 54.6% vs 59.7%), which removes the easiest excuse—but also means INDIGO's absolute numbers are solid, reproducible disease-control data, not a soft-trial artifact.
  • Safety ran in obexelimab's favor within its own trial: serious AEs 10.3% vs 18.6% on placebo; grade ≥3 infections lower on drug. In MITIGATE, serious AEs ran the other way (18% on inebilizumab vs 9% on placebo, per the published report).
  • Citi's January 5 note put its "ultra-bear" value—zero credit for IgG4-RD—at $18/share while raising its approval probability to 80%. The stock closed that day at $16.61, below a zero-indication scenario from a bank that still rated it Buy.
  • The October 2026 OLE data (12-month flare-free probability 0.93; vaccine responses preserved after a treatment-free window) support the durability and reversibility claims that are the actual commercial argument.

What the discount is honestly pricing

  • On the most like-for-like metric—annualized adjudicated flare rate—the drug arms are 0.34 (obexelimab) vs 0.10 (inebilizumab) with near-identical placebo rates. That gap cannot be waved away as trial design.
  • Complete remission: 37.1% vs MITIGATE's 57.4% treatment-free, flare-free remission. Different definitions, same direction.
  • Uplizna has been marketed since April 2025 with Amgen's infrastructure; obexelimab would launch ~2 years later as a second entrant with a weekly self-injection burden—52 injections a year against 2 maintenance infusions.
  • The balance sheet is pre-sold in slices: 5.5% of future obexelimab sales go to Royalty Pharma (at a 32.3% effective interest rate), Pharmakon holds a lien on substantially all assets including IP, and Xencor takes royalties up to the mid-teens in some regions plus up to $385M of sales milestones.
  • Burn is heavy: $132.4M of operating cash used in H1 2026, a $192.4M H1 net loss, and a CFO transition at end-September 2026, with interim cover from the President/COO and no permanent successor named.

03 / SCIENCE

Inhibit the B cell. Do not delete it.

IgG4-related disease is a fibroinflammatory condition driven by B-lineage cells: plasmablasts sustain autoantigen presentation, cytokine output, and fibroblast activation even though the IgG4 antibody itself is often a bystander. Obexelimab's bet is that co-engaging CD19 with the inhibitory FcγRIIB receptor can switch B cells off—reversibly—instead of depleting them, trading some depth of suppression for safety, vaccine competence, and at-home dosing.

01

B-lineage drive

Expanded plasmablasts and activated B cells present antigen to T cells, secrete profibrotic signals, and replenish short-lived plasma cells. Plasmablast burden tracks disease activity and relapse in cohort studies.

02

Organ inflammation → fibrosis

Multi-organ infiltration (pancreas, bile ducts, salivary/lacrimal glands, retroperitoneum, kidney) progresses to storiform fibrosis and irreversible organ damage if flares are not controlled; glucocorticoids work but relapse on taper and accumulate toxicity.

03

Co-engagement

Obexelimab binds CD19 on the B-cell surface and FcγRIIB with enhanced affinity via its engineered Fc (Xencor's XmAb platform). Tethering the two receptors recruits the ITIM inhibitory pathway and raises the B-cell activation threshold—without an effector-competent Fc that would kill the cell.

04

Inhibition, not depletion

B-cell counts stay above the lower limit of normal; immunoglobulins are largely preserved; the effect is reversible on discontinuation. That is the claimed difference from CD19/CD20 depleters (inebilizumab, rituximab): less hypogammaglobulinemia risk, retained vaccine responses, no prolonged B-cell aplasia—but also, plausibly, a shallower floor of disease suppression.

Mechanistic evidence

Proof the pathway engages in humans

The Xencor-era program showed dose-dependent inhibition of B-cell proliferation in Phase 1 (XmAb5871), and in a randomized SLE cohort, patients who maintained therapeutic exposure showed reduced loss of serologic/clinical response—evidence the CD19×FcγRIIB mechanism is biologically active in humans, though that trial's primary endpoint did not reach significance.

FACT FY2025 10-K clinical-history tables (§04); Xencor data as summarized therein.

The delivery angle

Weekly self-injection vs twice-yearly infusion

Obexelimab is 250 mg subcutaneous, weekly, at home; a single-dose prefilled autoinjector pen demonstrated pharmacokinetic bioequivalence in August 2026. Uplizna is intravenous, day 1 + day 15 loading, then once every six months in a clinic. INTERPRETATION The adherence math cuts both ways: 52 self-injections a year versus 2 supervised infusions—but zero infusion-chair time, no premedication visits, and no travel burden for a chronic, relapsing disease with an older, comorbid population.

04 / CLINICAL

One pivotal win, two open programs, two quiet write-downs

Obexelimab's clinical record is wider than the IgG4-RD headline: a positive MS MRI trial the company is not advancing with this molecule, an SLE Phase 2 reading out this quarter, a wAIHA program with proof of mechanism that will not be registered, and an RA program already discontinued.

Obexelimab in 60 seconds INTERPRETATION

Plain English. B cells help drive IgG4-RD even when they are not making the harmful antibody themselves. Obexelimab is an antibody that grabs a B cell with one hand (CD19) and presses the cell's own off-switch (FcγRIIB) with the other. The B cell survives but goes quiet, and because nothing is destroyed, stopping the drug lets the immune system come back—useful if a patient needs a vaccine or fights an infection.

Technical. A humanized bifunctional monoclonal antibody with an engineered Fc showing increased affinity for FcγRIIB and minimal effector function; co-ligation of CD19 and FcγRIIB delivers an ITIM-mediated inhibitory signal that suppresses B-cell activation, proliferation, and plasmablast differentiation without ADCC/CDC-mediated depletion. Licensed exclusively from Xencor in May 2021 (originated as XmAb5871).

FACT A Xencor-era Phase 1b/2a study was completed; Zenas discontinued RA development, citing the competitive landscape. Earlier INDIGO Phase 2 (Xencor, Boston, IV q2w): 80% of the 5 mg/kg cohort achieved ≥2-point Responder Index improvement by Day 169 and 53% (8/15) reached complete remission (RI 0, no glucocorticoids after Day 57, no flares)—the single-arm proof of concept that INDIGO was built on.

1 · Depth ceiling

If maximal B-cell inhibition without depletion cannot match depleter depth in harder populations (SLE nephritis, refractory IgG4-RD), the modality wins only the convenience segment.

2 · Adherence decay

Weekly self-injection for a disease with long quiet intervals invites discontinuation; real-world persistence for self-injected biologics decays materially in year one across immunology.

3 · Hypersensitivity / injection burden

INDIGO AEs more frequent on drug included hypersensitivity (16.5% vs 11.3%), arthralgia (19.6% vs 11.3%), diarrhea, and urticaria. Manageable in-trial; a label-and-launch friction if prominent.

4 · The SLE translation risk

The mechanism's best human evidence outside IgG4-RD is a non-significant primary endpoint with a supportive subgroup. SunStone is a genuine test, not a formality.

Safety signal in INDIGO's favor

FACT Serious AEs were less frequent on obexelimab than placebo (10.3% vs 18.6%), infections including grade 3 events were less frequent on drug per company reporting of the NEJM dataset, and there were no deaths on obexelimab (one cardiovascular death occurred on placebo). For balance, three malignancies were reported on obexelimab (3.1% — squamous-cell, prostate, and renal-cell carcinoma) versus none on placebo; investigators assessed them as unrelated to treatment.

What to watch in the label

Injection-site/hypersensitivity language, infection monitoring, and whether FDA asks for immunoglobulin monitoring despite non-depletion. Uplizna's label carries infection and infusion-reaction warnings; a cleaner obexelimab label is a real, if secondary, commercial asset.

What remains unknowable

GAP No head-to-head data exist against inebilizumab; long-term (multi-year) infection and malignancy rates for chronic FcγRIIB co-engagement are uncharacterized; OLE data are uncontrolled.

05 / ADJUDICATION

HR 0.44 versus HR 0.13: what the comparison proves, and what it doesn't

Both trials randomized IgG4-RD patients to drug or placebo for 52 weeks, tapered steroids to zero by Week 8, and counted adjudicated flares requiring rescue. That shared skeleton is why the market treated the numbers as comparable. This section grades the comparison element by element. Bottom line first: the honest differences explain only a small part of the effect-size gap. The rest is a real efficacy difference in MITIGATE's favor—and the obexelimab case has to be won on modality, safety, and durability, not on relitigating 0.44.

What the bear comparison gets right

  • Placebo arms behave nearly identically: annualized adjudicated flare rate 0.70 (INDIGO) vs 0.71 (MITIGATE); placebo flare incidence 54.6% vs 59.7%. The usual "different placebo, different trial" defense is weak here.
  • Drug-arm flare rates diverge hard: 0.34 vs 0.10 annualized; flare incidence 26.8% vs 10.3%; investigator-only INDIGO sensitivity HR (0.41) is no better than the adjudicated one.
  • Remission outcomes point the same way: complete remission 37.1% (INDIGO) vs 57.4% treatment-free, flare-free complete remission (MITIGATE).
  • Steroid exposure on drug was lower in MITIGATE in absolute terms (mean ~118 mg vs ~330 mg LS mean in INDIGO), even though both trials tapered to zero by Week 8.

Where the comparison overreaches

  • Populations differ at entry. Every INDIGO patient was enrolled in an active flare requiring steroid initiation/increase; single-organ, primarily fibrotic disease was excluded. MITIGATE enrolled newly diagnosed or recurrent patients who required glucocorticoids and had a confirmed history of organ involvement—not necessarily an active flare at randomization. INDIGO's population was arguably harder to keep flare-free (66.5% recurrent disease).
  • Remission definitions differ. INDIGO's CR required no adjudicated flare and no flare treatment, plus a Responder Index of 0 or a physician-global VAS of 0 mm; MITIGATE's headline CR was treatment-free and flare-free. The 37.1% vs 57.4% gap overstates the definitional difference—but does not erase it.
  • Sample size. INDIGO (N=194) is 44% larger than MITIGATE (N=135); confidence intervals on MITIGATE's HR (0.06–0.28) and INDIGO's (0.277–0.711) all but touch—"different drugs, different depths" is real, but neither estimate is precise.
  • Within-trial safety direction differs. SAEs favored drug in INDIGO (10.3% vs 18.6%) and disfavored drug in MITIGATE (18% vs 9%). If efficacy is compared across trials, safety must be too—and it cuts the other way.
The verdict. INTERPRETATION Treat the cross-trial gap as genuine but inflated. On flare suppression—the endpoint class both trials share—inebilizumab is the stronger drug in its dataset, and no amount of design exegesis turns HR 0.44 into HR 0.13. What the comparison cannot settle is the commercial question: in a ~20,000-patient U.S. diagnosed population (company estimate) with an older, comorbid base, how much share a reversible, non-depleting, at-home weekly injection takes from a twice-yearly clinic infusion with deeper suppression and a two-year head start. That is a launch-execution and label question, and it is exactly what the May 2027 PDUFA and the first four quarters of launch will answer. No head-to-head claim is made anywhere in this dossier.

13 / RISKS

Ranked red flags

The first four can move the stock on a dated event. The rest determine how much of any success the common shareholder actually keeps.

  1. Second-entrant depth gapOn matched trial skeletons, inebilizumab's flare suppression (HR 0.13, annualized 0.10) is materially deeper than obexelimab's (HR 0.44, 0.34) with near-identical placebo behavior; Uplizna has a ~2-year launch head start and Amgen's infrastructure.
  2. PDUFA binary — May 27, 2027Any CRL, label narrowing, or monitoring requirement that erases the convenience argument re-rates the equity toward the bear range; no advisory committee has been announced, which cuts both ways.
  3. SunStone dependenceThe SLE leg rests on a mechanism whose only prior randomized SLE trial missed its primary endpoint; a Q4 2026 miss removes the second indication and strands the valuation on IgG4-RD alone at ~$1.5B EV.
  4. Encumbered economics5.5% of obexelimab sales to Royalty Pharma (32.3% effective rate), Xencor royalties to the mid-teens plus ~$450M of remaining milestones, BMS's Asian carve-out, and a secured lender with a lien on the IP: gross sales will not equal Zenas economics.
  5. Burn and creep dilution~$66M/quarter operating cash use, an active ATM ($96.8M left), converts struck at ~$26.50, and 2.0M contingent InnoCare shares; FD share count is ~81M on an if-converted basis versus 63.3M basic.
  6. Conditional runway"Through Q2 2029" assumes $150M of milestone-linked inflows ($75M Royalty Pharma approval payment + $75M Pharmakon tranche); a delayed approval pulls the cash wall forward.
  7. Orelabrutinib capital intensityTwo progressive-MS Phase 3s plus up to $723M of InnoCare milestones compete for the same cash that funds the obexelimab launch; progressive-MS timelines are long and binary.
  8. Adherence realityWeekly self-injection, indefinitely, for a relapsing disease with quiet intervals; INDIGO hypersensitivity AEs (16.5% vs 11.3% on placebo) hint at the friction the label and launch must manage.
  9. CFO transition mid-reviewCFO Jennifer Fox moves to a strategic-advisor role at end-September 2026; the President/COO leads finance on an interim basis and no permanent successor has been named in reviewed disclosures—during BLA review and launch build-out.
  10. Stranded MS dataA 95%-positive Phase 2 (MoonStone) with no announced registrational path is either unpriced optionality or dead spend; a year of silence since the readout argues the market should not pay for it yet.
14 / OPEN

The evidence that would change the view

A deep dive should end with falsifiers, not a verdict. These are the highest-value missing pieces as of October 20, 2026.

01

SunStone's actual result

Overall-population vs biomarker-population outcome, effect size, and whether Zenas self-funds or partners SLE Phase 3.

02

The label negotiation

AdCom scheduling, flare-prevention claim scope, infection/immunoglobulin monitoring language, and autoinjector inclusion at launch.

03

Royalty Pharma's dead tranche

The undisclosed conditions on the $75M INDIGO-milestone payment the company says it is "not currently eligible" for—waivable, expired, or renegotiable?

04

Per-organ INDIGO depth

Response by organ system and any fibrosis-regression evidence in the full NEJM/OLE datasets—what determines use in fibrotic, high-risk patients where Uplizna's depth matters most.

05

Launch economics

Net price versus Uplizna, salesforce size, and the CFO succession—who owns the launch budget, and how much of the $674M is earmarked for commercial build.

06

InnoCare trigger status

Whether Monarch's initiation has triggered (or will trigger) the $25M cash + 2.0M-share milestone, and the real all-in cost curve of the two progressive-MS Phase 3s.

INTERPRETATION

Current research posture

The market's January verdict conflated two questions. On efficacy depth, the discount is earned: obexelimab is, on the available cross-trial evidence, the weaker flare suppressant. On everything else—probability of approval (a hit pivotal trial, an accepted BLA, a hard PDUFA date), safety direction, durability in the OLE, and the commercial value of a reversible at-home option in a 20,000-patient U.S. market—the $15–20 trough priced a failure that the primary sources do not support, and even the recovered $28.18 capitalizes only a fraction of consensus expectations. The honest residual risks are not scientific; they are structural: a pre-sold top line, a heavy burn, and two dated binaries (SunStone, Q4 2026; PDUFA, May 27, 2027) that will settle most of this within seven months.
