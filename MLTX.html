03 / SCIENCE

Two cytokines, one small protein, and a penetration claim still awaiting proof

HS is a neutrophil-driven, tunnel-forming inflammatory disease in which IL-17A and IL-17F are both upregulated. Sonelokimab blocks all three IL-17A/F dimers with a molecule roughly a quarter of an antibody’s size. The pharmacology is sound; the differentiating claim — deeper tissue penetration from the nanobody format — remains a claim, because no human tissue-exposure comparison has been published.

01

Follicular occlusion

HS begins around hair follicles in intertriginous skin; rupture spills keratin and bacteria into the dermis and triggers intense innate inflammation.

02

IL-17 amplification

IL-17A and IL-17F (as A/A, A/F and F/F dimers) drive neutrophil recruitment and keratinocyte activation. Lesional HS skin overexpresses both — the rationale for dual blockade over IL-17A alone.

03

Abscesses and tunnels

Neutrophilic abscesses coalesce into draining tunnels (sinus tracts) and fibrosis — the structural damage HiSCR counts and IHS4 weights most heavily, and the stage at which response is hardest to win.

04

Dual blockade

Sonelokimab binds IL-17A and IL-17F with high affinity, neutralizing all three dimers — the same ligand pair as bimekizumab, in a ~40 kDa format instead of a ~150 kDa IgG.

Format

Three VHH domains, deliberately built

FACT Sonelokimab (originator code M1095; in-licensed from Merck Healthcare KGaA) is a humanized trivalent Nanobody®: two binding domains against IL-17A and IL-17F, plus a central anti-albumin domain that extends half-life and is intended to enrich the molecule at sites of inflammatory edema, where albumin extravasates. Flexible glycine-serine linkers join the domains; the drug is produced in Pichia pastoris yeast. Clinical dosing is a single 1 mL subcutaneous injection — 120 mg monthly in HS after a 4-dose induction, 60 mg in PsA.

FACT Company scientific description, Q2 2026 release (8-K Ex-99.1, Aug 10, 2026); structure first described in Papp et al., Lancet 2021.

Claims vs evidence

“Tissue penetration” is plausible, not demonstrated

GAP No published study measures sonelokimab concentrations in human HS lesions against a monoclonal antibody comparator, and no head-to-head trial exists against bimekizumab. The indirect evidence is real but circumstantial: deep-response rates at Week 52 (26.0% IHS4-100, i.e., complete resolution of abscesses, nodules and draining tunnels) are the kind of outcome a penetration advantage would predict — and are also what any potent IL-17A/F blocker might produce with a year of dosing. INTERPRETATION Treat format superiority as unproven; treat dual-ligand efficacy as proven at the class level by Bimzelx.

04 / CLINICAL

VELA: the first HS program to bet on HiSCR75 — and nearly lose on arithmetic

MoonLake designed VELA-1 and VELA-2 (838 patients) around HiSCR75 at Week 16 — a higher response bar than any prior Phase 3 primary — with two pre-specified ways of handling patients who left, flared, or took rescue therapy. The drug cleared the bar in one trial under both strategies, and in the second trial under one. Everything since has been an argument about which strategy should count.

The estimand fight in 60 seconds INTERPRETATION

Plain English. In HS trials, some patients quit, need surgery, or take rescue antibiotics. The composite strategy scores every such patient as a failure, whatever their lesions did. The treatment-policy strategy scores what actually happened to their lesions. VELA-2’s placebo arm had an unusual cluster of these “intercurrent events” alongside a genuinely high placebo response — so the composite math failed (p=0.053) while the treatment-policy math passed (p=0.033). Regulators had seen both strategies in advance; which one anchors the label is now an FDA review question, not a data question.

Technical. Primary endpoint: HiSCR75 at Week 16 (≥75% reduction in abscess + inflammatory nodule count, no increase in abscess or draining-tunnel count). Dosing: 120 mg SC every other week to Week 6 (4 induction doses), then monthly; placebo crossed to sonelokimab at Week 16; assessments to Week 52, then a 2-year open-label extension. Randomization ~2:1 (VELA-1: 283 SLK / 138 PBO; VELA-2: 276 / 141).

1 · The label could price in the miss

FDA agreed the package can establish effectiveness; it did not pre-agree the label. A label built on VELA-1 alone, without the adolescent indication or with the composite analysis featured, would narrow the commercial claim set.

2 · Placebo response is drifting up class-wide

Recent HS trials (VELA-2 placebo HiSCR50 43.0%; povorcitinib placebo ~29–30% at an easier Week 12 bar) show rising placebo rates. Any future trial MoonLake runs — including label-expansion studies — faces the same headwind.

3 · Adolescent data are thin by design

VELA-TEEN is a 35-patient open-label PK/safety study. Interim Week 24: ~68% HiSCR75, ~45% HiSCR100 (n=22). Supportive, not definitive; the BLA asks FDA to include ages 12+ on this plus adult efficacy extrapolation.

4 · Durability beyond a year is unproven

Week 52 is the longest controlled exposure. Bimzelx already has 2- and 3-year HS datasets public. The VELA open-label extension (N=835) will take years to close that gap.

05 / BLINDED

IZAR-1 “hit everything” — except the comparison that decides value

The August 10, 2026 IZAR-1 release is the most unusual disclosure in the story: under an unblinding protocol agreed with FDA, MoonLake published the winning drug arm’s absolute response rates and withheld the placebo arm entirely, because the trial is still running to Week 52. The numbers look strong. They are also, by construction, unscoreable — and that gap is the H1 2027 binary.

“Consistent with the unblinding protocol defined with the FDA, topline disclosure at Week 16 includes absolute response levels and endpoint outcomes for the sonelokimab 60 mg with induction arm. Comparative analyses versus placebo and detailed treatment arm data remain blinded until completion of the Phase 3 program.”
MoonLake IZAR-1 topline release · Aug 10, 2026 (8-K Ex-99.1)
FACT

What was disclosed

60 mg with-induction arm, Week 16: ACR50 (primary) 42.1%; ACR20 66.5%; MDA 41.2%; PASI90 61% in skin-involved patients; HAQ-DI −0.427; SF-36 PCS +6.54. All clinical endpoints met. Blinded safety: consistent with prior studies, no new signals, low dropout.

FACT

What was not

No placebo rates, no p-values versus placebo, no data for the 60 mg without-induction arm, no per-arm N in the release, no ACR70, enthesitis, or dactylitis detail. The word “met” for the primary endpoint implies the pre-specified comparison succeeded — but the margin is invisible.

FACT

The best placebo anchor

In the published Phase 2 ARGO trial (same molecule, same 60 mg with-induction regimen), placebo ACR50 at Week 12 was 20.0% and drug 46.3%. If IZAR-1’s placebo behaves like ARGO’s, the hidden delta is ~20 pts — competitive with any approved PsA biologic. That is an anchor, not a measurement.

GAP

Why it can still go wrong

Phase 3 PsA placebo ACR50 rates have drifted upward across the industry, and IZAR-1 enrolled 960 patients across three arms — a different population from ARGO’s 207. A placebo print near 30% would halve the delta, reframe IZAR-1 as “me-too IL-17,” and shift all value onto IZAR-2’s harder TNF-refractory test.

06 / SEP 2025

Autopsy of a −90% day: what the VELA print actually said

The September 28, 2025 release contained a genuine disappointment wrapped in a statistical technicality, priced as a program failure. Reconstructing it matters because the same three readings are still available today — and the evidence accumulated since discriminates between them.

What the print contained

  • VELA-1: every primary and key secondary endpoint significant under both analysis strategies; HiSCR75 composite delta +17 pts, p<0.001.
  • VELA-2: primary missed under the composite strategy — delta +9 pts, p=0.053 — and hit under the pre-specified treatment-policy strategy, 35.9% vs 25.6%, p=0.033.
  • Drug-arm HiSCR75 was nearly identical in both trials (34.8% / 35.9%, treatment policy). The variance was in VELA-2’s placebo arm: 25.6% vs a 13–18% historical Phase 3 range, compounded by intercurrent events scored as failures under the composite.
  • Safety was clean and class-typical: candidiasis 7.3% vs 0.4% was the only material imbalance; no IBD, no SI/B, no MACE.
  • The stock had closed at $61.99 (~$4.0B market value) the prior Friday, pricing near-flawless execution.

What the print could not tell anyone

  • Whether FDA would accept a filing built on one clean trial plus one strategy-dependent trial — the only question that actually determined HS value.
  • Whether VELA-2’s placebo rate was noise, site/population mix, or a real feature of the trial’s conduct.
  • Whether Week 16 was even the right frame: HS responses to IL-17 blockade deepen for a year, and the release itself noted improvement beyond Week 16.
  • What any of it implied for PsA, where the same molecule was already in two Phase 3 trials with a different endpoint culture (ACR) and a published positive Phase 2.
How the question resolved. FACT On December 15, 2025, MoonLake took the package to a Type B meeting. FDA’s answer (disclosed January 8, 2026): substantial evidence of effectiveness can be established without additional HS trials, from VELA-1, VELA-2 and MIRA; FDA advised including MIRA in the submission and VELA-2 regardless of its evidentiary role, to inform the safety profile. Week 52 data (June 21, 2026) then showed both trials converging at ~67% HiSCR75 with crossover patients replicating the trajectory. The BLA followed on September 30, 2026. INTERPRETATION The September 2025 price was a probability-weighted regulatory death sentence. The realized path was the benign branch of every uncertainty the print left open.

Mandatory cross-trial caveat. The numbers above come from different trials, estimands, imputation rules, and eras of placebo response; none of these drugs has faced another head-to-head in HS. Sonelokimab’s higher-threshold data (HiSCR75/100, IHS4-100) are genuinely distinctive as a dataset, and the Week 52 depth is at least comparable to bimekizumab’s long-term trajectory — but “best-in-class” remains a marketing claim until a label, a launch, and persistence data exist. Also in the field: adalimumab and biosimilars anchor the cheap end (PIONEER HiSCR50 41.8% / 58.9% at Week 12 vs 26.0% / 27.6%); AbbVie’s lutikizumab (IL-1α/β) posted a 59.5% vs 35.0% HiSCR50 in a TNF-failed Phase 2 and is in Phase 3; Merck’s tulisokibart (TL1A) showed striking small Phase 2b numbers in 2026. The window in which IL-17A/F differentiation alone suffices is not permanent.

13 / RISKS

Ranked red flags

Ranked by capacity to destroy value on a near-term timeline, not by probability alone. The first three are the dossier’s core: the review record, the blinded placebo, and the replication trial.

  1. VELA-2’s miss lives in the reviewThe composite primary failure (p=0.053) is a permanent part of the BLA. FDA agreed the package can establish effectiveness — it did not agree the label will feature HiSCR75, both trials, or adolescents. Label negotiation is where the miss collects its toll.
  2. The blinded placebo is a true unknownIZAR-1’s value rests on a comparison nobody outside the blinded team has seen. ARGO anchors placebo ACR50 at 20.0%; a print near 30% halves the delta and reprices PsA from “second franchise” to “me-too.”
  3. IZAR-2 is the harder roomTNF-refractory patients, two dose regimens, and a risankizumab arm that invites direct comparison with the IL-23 benchmark. Enrollment completion was guided for Q3 2026 and is unconfirmed — slippage compounds the risk.
  4. Bimzelx has a two-year head start and UCB’s balance sheet€1.53B H1 2026 sales across indications, ≥€7B peak ambition, 3-year HS data already presented. MoonLake must build a salesforce and payer access from zero against that machine.
  5. An oral is comingPovorcitinib hit both STOP-HS primaries with deepening 54-week responses (HiSCR75 up to 57%). A daily pill with JAK baggage still beats injections for a large patient segment — and it may reach market on a similar timeline.
  6. Class safety scales with successCandidiasis at 7.3% is priced in. What is not testable yet: IBD emergence in unscreened real-world use, and the SI/B scrutiny the class carries from bimekizumab’s label history — at millions of patient-years, not 838 trial patients.
  7. Single-asset, single-mechanism concentrationEvery program is sonelokimab; every indication shares the IL-17A/F safety and CMC chain (one yeast-expression platform, $172.1M of CMO commitments at Jun 30, 2026).
  8. Burn and dilution are structural~$61M/quarter operating cash use; share count +32% in seven months; a launch to fund in 2027. Runway to mid-2028 is guided, but a launch-year raise at weak prices is a base-case feature, not a tail.
  9. Royalty and milestone stackLow-to-mid-teen percent of net sales to Merck KGaA / RCT plus up to €299.6M of milestones. Permanently compresses margins and takeout value relative to wholly-owned peers.
  10. Insider and governance opticsCSO sold at $20 into the offering window; no insider buying identified in 2026; Q1 2026’s voluntary option cancellations ($4.8M accelerated expense) are unexplained beyond the filing. None is disqualifying; together they argue against paying for alignment.

INTERPRETATION

Current research posture

At ~$441M of enterprise value, the market is paying for the HS filing as if VELA-2’s p=0.053 were still the live question. It isn’t: FDA answered that question in January, and the BLA is in. The live question is one nobody can currently answer — what IZAR-1’s placebo arm did — plus the ordinary execution risk of a first launch against UCB. The setup is asymmetric in a specific way: the downside is cushioned by $5.13/share of net cash and an approvable lead asset, while the upside requires the blinded data to be as good as the unblinded half suggests. That is a coherent reason the stock is cheap. It is not proof that it is mispriced.
