02 / SCIENCE

Treg expansion by design—and the efficacy question

Plain-language mechanism

FACTRezpegaldesleukin is a recombinant human interleukin-2 (IL-2) that has been chemically modified (PEGylated) so that it preferentially signals through the trimeric high-affinity IL-2 receptor (IL-2Rα/β/γ, CD25/CD122/CD132) — the receptor form constitutively and densely expressed on regulatory T cells (Tregs) — rather than the dimeric intermediate-affinity receptor (IL-2Rβ/γ) found on effector T cells and NK cells. The net pharmacology is selective expansion and activation of Tregs, the immune system's "brakes," which suppress the pathogenic effector T cells driving autoimmune attack. [R1, R9]

INTERPRETATIONThe design solves the two classic problems of native IL-2 therapy: (1) a very short half-life requiring frequent dosing, and (2) at higher doses, stimulation of effector cells that worsens inflammation or causes toxicity. By biasing toward the Treg receptor, rezpeg aims to "restore immune balance" rather than broadly suppress immunity — the company calls it a "resolution therapeutic." [R1]

What the academic literature says (beyond company materials)

The Treg/IL-2 hypothesis is not a company invention — it rests on a deep literature:

  • FACTKlatzmann & Abbas, Nature Reviews Immunology 15:283–294 (2015) — the foundational review arguing that low-dose IL-2 can selectively expand Tregs and treat autoimmune/inflammatory disease, because Tregs constitutively express the high-affinity IL-2Rα chain (CD25) while resting effector cells do not. [R10]
  • FACTCastela et al., JAMA Dermatology 150:748–751 (2014) — low-dose recombinant IL-2 promoted Tregs and showed activity in alopecia areata patients, directly supporting the AA indication rationale. [R10]
  • FACTDILT1D / DILfrequency studies (Hartemann et al., Nature Communications 2022) — adaptive dose-finding of low-dose aldesleukin in type 1 diabetes established the dosing window (0.2–0.47 × 10⁶ IU/m²) that raises Tregs 20–50% without expanding effector T cells; supports the T1D program's biological premise. [R10]
  • FACTHe et al., Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases 79:141–149 (2020) — randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of low-dose IL-2 in systemic lupus erythematosus met its endpoints, showing Treg expansion can translate clinically in autoimmunity. [R10]
  • FACTKoreth et al., NEJM 365:2055–2066 (2011) — low-dose IL-2 expanded Tregs and produced responses in steroid-refractory chronic graft-versus-host disease. [R10]
  • FACTProtein-engineering literature (e.g., Nature Communications 2022, s41467-022-31130-9) — documents the now-standard strategies rezpeg follows: weakening IL-2Rβ engagement (by mutation or masking, here via PEGylation) to force dependence on IL-2Rα, increasing Treg selectivity and half-life. [R10]

Why it could fail — the honest scientific risks

  • FACTTreg expansion ≠ clinical efficacy, proven twice. Rezpeg itself failed the Phase 2 ISLAND trial in SLE (Feb 2023, missed SLEDAI-2K primary) under Lilly. [R11] Independently, the LUPIL-2 trial of low-dose IL-2 in SLE (Humrich et al., Ann Rheum Dis 2022) missed its primary endpoint despite the biological rationale. [R10] INTERPRETATION — The mechanism can be pharmacologically "working" (Tregs expand) while the disease doesn't care.
  • FACTIndication-specific biology. SLE failure does not predict AD/AA outcomes — but it proves the drug's effect is not indication-agnostic. AD and AA are the indications with positive data; the T1D leg has no human efficacy data for rezpeg at all. [R11]
  • INTERPRETATIONResponder-rate ceiling. Phase 2b AD EASI-75 of 42% (vs 17% placebo) leaves a majority of treated patients as non-responders at 16 weeks; the Phase 3 primary is the stricter IGA 0/1 (clear/almost-clear) at 24 weeks, where the Phase 2b hit rate was 20% vs 8%. If placebo IGA 0/1 drifts above ~10–12% in a registrational population, the delta compresses.
  • INTERPRETATIONTolerability over years. Injection-site reactions in 69.7% of patients are mostly mild and self-resolving, but chronic q2w→q4w/q12w subcutaneous dosing with persistent ISRs is a real-world adherence question for a maintenance therapy, and eosinophilia (7.8% vs 2.7%) bears watching in longer Phase 3 exposure.
  • GAPNo published head-to-head data versus any approved AD or AA therapy exists, and the Phase 2b populations were biologic/JAK-naïve — the Phase 3 AD-3 study in treatment-experienced patients will test the mechanism in a harder population with no prior readout.

04 / TRIALS

REZOLVE-AD: the dataset Phase 3 is built on

REZOLVE-AD — Phase 2b in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (completed)

FACTGlobal, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; n=393 biologic/JAK-naïve patients, randomized 3:3:3:2 to rezpeg 24 µg/kg q2w (n=104), 18 µg/kg q2w (n=106), 24 µg/kg q4w (n=110), or placebo q2w (n=73). Primary and key secondaries at Week 16. Patients with ≥50% EASI improvement were re-randomized 1:1 to q4w or q12w maintenance through Week 52. NCT06136741. [R13, R1]

FACTAll three dose arms met the primary endpoint (18 µg/kg q2w: 58%, p<0.0001; 24 µg/kg q4w: 53%, p=0.0002). Significant EASI separation appeared by Week 2 (24 µg/kg arms) and Week 4 (all arms), with responses deepening through Week 16. Efficacy was consistent across baseline severity (vIGA-AD 3 vs 4) and geography. Results published in The Lancet (Aug 2026). [R13, R14]

FACTSafety (16-week induction): ISRs in 69.7% of rezpeg patients (99.6% mild/moderate; 0.2% severe; <1% discontinued for ISR). TEAEs >5% vs placebo (n=320 vs 73): eosinophilia 7.8% vs 2.7%, pyrexia 6.3% vs 2.7%, headache 6.3% vs 4.1%, arthralgia 5.0% vs 1.4%. TEAEs excluding ISRs: 60.3% drug vs 57.5% placebo. Serious AEs: 5 (1.6%) drug vs 0 placebo; discontinuations 5.6% vs 0. No increased conjunctivitis, oral ulcers, or infections (incl. oral herpes). No deaths. [R13]

FACTMaintenance (EADV, Oct 1, 2026): q4w and q12w regimens sustained EASI-75/90, vIGA-AD, and itch responses through Week 52. EASI-100 conversions: q4w 4%→22%, q12w 9%→18% (all re-randomized, wk16→wk52); among wk16 responders, q4w 6%→30%, q12w 14%→27%. Safety through 52 weeks "favorable and consistent." [R2]

INTERPRETATIONThis is a clean, dose-ordered Phase 2b win with biomarker support (TARC/CCL17, periostin, MDC/CCL22, IL-19 reductions; early biomarker changes correlated with Week 16 EASI, p<0.001, drug arm only). The open statistical question is how much of the 31% placebo EASI response washes into the 24-week IGA 0/1 endpoint in Phase 3.

REZOLVE-AA — Phase 2b in severe-to-very-severe alopecia areata (completed)

FACTGlobal, n=92, randomized 3:3:2 to rezpeg 24 µg/kg q2w, 18 µg/kg q2w, or placebo q2w. Primary endpoint: mean % SALT reduction at Week 36. NCT06340360. [R15, R1]

⚠ Critical nuance — primary endpoint missed in the pre-specified analysis

FACTIn the modified intent-to-treat population, mean SALT reductions were 28.2% (24 µg/kg), 30.3% (18 µg/kg) vs 11.2% placebo — p=0.186 and p=0.121, not significant. Statistical significance (p=0.049 / p=0.042; ~30% vs 6%) was achieved only after excluding 4 patients with major baseline eligibility violations — a post-hoc analysis. SALT30 responder rates: 48.9% / 45.7% / 19.1%. Well tolerated; 1.4% discontinuation rate. [R15]

FACT52-week extension (reported Aug 2026): 31 patients with SALT>20 at Week 36 continued blinded treatment to Week 52; new SALT≤20 responses: 29% (18 µg/kg) and 31% (24 µg/kg) vs 0% placebo. Off-treatment durability (EADV Oct 2026, n=8): SALT≤20 maintained in 6/8 (75%) at 4 months and 5/8 (63%) at 6 months off treatment; SALT≤10 deepened from 7% (end of treatment) to 19% at 6 months off treatment. [R2]

INTERPRETATIONThe AA dataset is directionally encouraging — consistent separation at all timepoints, dose-ordered responder rates, and durability that no approved AA therapy has demonstrated — but it is thin: a missed primary, a 4-patient post-hoc rescue, and an n=8 durability subset. This is the highest-variance leg of the story.

ZENITH-AD — Phase 3 program (enrolling)

FACTThree registrational studies, ~510 patients each (~1,530 total), ages 12+, across North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific. AD-1 and AD-2 (biologic/JAK-naïve) initiated July 2026; AD-3 (prior systemic biologic and/or JAK inhibitor treatment-experienced) initiated October 2026. Dosing: rezpeg 24 µg/kg q2w for a 24-week induction; responders (EASI-75 and/or IGA 0/1 at Week 24) re-randomized 2:2:1 to monthly, quarterly, or placebo for a 28-week maintenance; non-responders enter an open-label escape arm. [R5]

FACTPrimary endpoint: IGA 0/1 response at Week 24 (US); co-primary IGA 0/1 + EASI-75 at Week 24 ex-US. Key secondaries: EASI-75, EASI-90, Itch NRS ≥4 reduction; prespecified ACQ-5 asthma assessment (~25% of AD patients have comorbid asthma). Initial topline data expected mid-2028; BLA submission targeted 2029. [R5]

INTERPRETATIONDesign choices to note: (1) a 24-week primary (vs 16-week in most AD registrational trials) gives the slow-building Treg mechanism more time — but also more time for placebo drift; (2) AD-3 tests the drug where no data exists (treatment-experienced), a deliberate regulatory bet that broadens the label if it works; (3) the maintenance re-randomization to placebo will generate the withdrawal/durability data payers want.

ZENITH-AA — Phase 3 (planned)

FACTn=850, severe-to-very-severe AA; primary endpoint SALT≤20 at Week 52; initiation expected early 2027; company guides potential BLA in 2029 if positive. [R2, R16]

T1D — NIH/TrialNet Phase 2 (RESET T1D)

FACTNIH-sponsored, TrialNet-conducted, Nektar supplies drug + PK support; n=66, ages 8–45, new-onset T1D (≤100 days), 2:1 randomized to rezpeg q14d × 26 weeks vs placebo; efficacy = MMTT stimulated C-peptide AUC over 12 months; secondaries: HbA1c, insulin use, PK/PD. Company guidance: initial data in 2027. [R12]
