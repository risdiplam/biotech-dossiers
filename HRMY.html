02 / SCIENCE

Three pharmacologies: wake-promotion, orexin, serotonin

Three distinct pharmacologies: histamine H3 inverse agonism (pitolisant), orexin-2 agonism (BP-205), serotonin 5-HT2 modulation (EPX-100).

2.1 Pitolisant — selective histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse agonist

Plain language. Histamine neurons in the brain's tuberomammillary nucleus act as a "wakefulness switch." H3 receptors are presynaptic brakes on those neurons. Pitolisant releases the brake: it blocks/inversely agonizes H3 autoreceptors, increasing histamine synthesis and release, which in turn boosts other wake-promoting transmitters — dopamine, norepinephrine, acetylcholine — in cortex. Because the dopamine increase is cortical rather than via the nucleus accumbens reward circuit, pitolisant is not a scheduled controlled substance, unlike stimulants and oxybates. FACT (Kollb-Sielecka et al., Drug Des Devel Ther 2017, PMC6124464; Dauvilliers et al. pediatric Ph3 write-up)

Clinical validation. Approved August 2019 for EDS in adult narcolepsy; cataplexy indication added later. Pivotal trials (HARMONY program) showed ESS reduction and weekly cataplexy-rate reduction vs placebo at 5–40 mg/day; one trial tested non-inferiority vs modafinil 100–400 mg on ESS (not shown non-inferior on ESS, but superior on cataplexy per reviews). Pediatric Ph3 (NCT02611687, ages 6–17, n≈110): Ullanlinna Narcolepsy Scale LS-mean difference −3.7 (95% CI −6.4 to −1.0, p=0.007), TEAEs 31% vs 34% placebo. FACT (PMID 35774905 / PMC9239364 review; NeurologyLive pediatric Ph3 summary)

Why pitolisant could fail — mechanism-level. (1) It is symptomatic, not disease-targeted: in narcolepsy type 1 the root lesion is loss of orexin (hypocretin) neurons; pitolisant amplifies downstream wake circuitry without replacing orexin signaling. Orexin-2 agonists now address the lesion directly. INTERPRETATION (2) Effect sizes are moderate: ESS deltas of a few points, and the modafinil non-inferiority miss on ESS caps the efficacy narrative. FACT (3) Dose ceiling: the approved max is 35.6 mg/day; higher doses were historically limited by GI tolerability — which is exactly what HBS-301's enteric coating is trying to solve, an unproven formulation bet. INTERPRETATION (4) QT-prolongation interactions flagged in pediatric data warrant arrhythmia-risk monitoring — a label-level overhang for dose escalation. FACT (NeurologyLive)

2.2 BP-205 — orexin-2 receptor (OX2R) agonist

Plain language. In narcolepsy type 1, the brain's orexin neurons die. An OX2R agonist is a chemical substitute for the missing orexin — it directly stimulates the wakefulness receptor instead of working around it. Licensed from Bioprojet (as BP1.15205) for narcolepsy and other CNS indications. FACT (10-K FY2025, TradingView summary) Phase 1 SAD (n=72 healthy volunteers, 0.2–6 mg): Tmax 30–75 min, ~25-hour half-life supporting once-daily dosing, dose-proportional exposure, no serious/severe TEAEs, no cardiovascular/hepatic/visual signals; most common AEs headache (10%), fatigue (4%), diarrhea (3%). FACT (Q2 2026 PR; NeurologyLive) Company claims 0.015 nM potency — ~160× Orzeyful's 2.5 nM — framing it as potentially best-in-class. UNVERIFIED (company statement via PharmaVoice; in-vitro potency ≠ clinical superiority)

Why BP-205 could fail. (1) Years behind: Takeda's oveporexton is already FDA-approved; Alkermes' alixorexton is in Phase 3; Lilly paid $6.3B for Centessa's orexin pipeline — BP-205 enters Phase 2 in mid-2027 at best. FACT (2) Class safety is still being written: insomnia and urinary urgency/frequency are the signature on-target AEs of OX2R agonists (seen with oveporexton); BP-205's SAD was in healthy volunteers, not patients. INTERPRETATION (3) The ultra-potency claim cuts both ways — a 0.015 nM agonist has a narrow therapeutic window, and potency says nothing about selectivity, tachyphylaxis, or the visual-safety questions that have shadowed the class. INTERPRETATION

2.3 EPX-100 (clemizole HCl) — central 5-HT2 serotonin receptor modulator

Plain language. Clemizole is a first-generation antihistamine that was shelved decades ago — and rediscovered by accident. Baraban's lab screened 320 compounds in zebrafish carrying the Dravet-causing scn1Lab sodium-channel mutation; clemizole was the only hit that abolished both behavioral and electrographic seizures. Follow-up binding work showed the anti-seizure effect comes not from histamine blockade (49 other antihistamines failed) but from serotonin-2 receptor modulation — the same pathway implicated for fenfluramine (Fintepla) and lorcaserin in Dravet. FACT (Baraban et al., Nat Commun 2013;4:2410; Griffin et al., Brain 2017;140:669–683; Frontiers in Pharmacol review) Oral liquid, dosed twice daily. FDA orphan + rare pediatric disease designations for both Dravet and LGS. FACT

Why EPX-100 could fail. (1) The entire human thesis rests on zebrafish + a small open-label extension (n=18 with ≥6 months exposure; median ~50% seizure reduction). Small, uncontrolled, enriched — classic setup for a Phase 3 placebo surprise. INTERPRETATION (2) Dravet/LGS trials have a graveyard of plausible mechanisms; seizure-count endpoints are noisy and concomitant AEDs confound. INTERPRETATION (3) Serotonergic precedent is double-edged: fenfluramine works but carries valvular/pulmonary-hypertension monitoring baggage; investors will demand clean cardiac data. INTERPRETATION

04 / TRIAL DESIGN

Five Phase 3s and a PDUFA: the 2027 design package

Endpoints, statistical context, and readouts to date for each binary event.

4.1 ONSTRIDE-1 — HBS-301 (pitolisant up to 60 mg) in adult narcolepsy guidance

Phase 3 of high-dose enteric-coated pitolisant tablets (multiple strengths up to 60 mg vs the approved 35.6 mg max) for EDS and other narcolepsy symptoms. FACT (Oct 1, 2026 company PR; NCT07675135) Design details (randomization, endpoints, powering): not located in sources reviewed. GAP The strategic logic: enteric coating is intended to move the GI-tolerability ceiling so a near-doubling of dose is feasible; efficacy at higher exposure is the open question — pitolisant's approved dose-response was established only to 35.6 mg. INTERPRETATION Topline guided 2027; company targets PDUFA in 2028. FACT (Q2 2026 PR)

4.2 ONSTRIDE-2 — HBS-301 in adult idiopathic hypersomnia guidance

Phase 3 of high-dose pitolisant in IH, topline 2027. FACT (NCT07500090) This is the highest-skepticism readout in the portfolio: standard-dose pitolisant already ran a full Phase 3 in IH — INTUNE (NCT05156047) — a placebo-controlled, double-blind randomized-withdrawal study that missed its primary endpoint (ESS during the 4-week double-blind withdrawal; topline Oct 2023; stock fell 33% to $20.23). The open-label period had looked strong (n=213 enrolled; 83% of completers were ESS responders with mean −9.4 points), and later analyses showed significance on prespecified secondaries (IHSS, Sleep Inertia Questionnaire), but the registrational primary failed. FACT (company PR Oct 2023; NeurologyLive; Sleep 2025 abstract) The bull case for ONSTRIDE-2 is that higher exposure (60 mg) converts the INTUNE "positive trends" into a statistically significant primary. The bear case is that IH pathophysiology (not orexin-deficient, unlike NT1) simply doesn't respond to H3 inverse agonism at any tolerable dose — and a parallel design risks a parallel outcome. INTERPRETATION Design details not located. GAP

4.3 TEMPO — pitolisant in Prader-Willi syndrome (NCT06366464) guidance

Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter, global; n=134 patients aged ≥6 years with genetically confirmed PWS and EDS; 1:1 pitolisant vs placebo QD; up to 45-day screening/baseline, double-blind period with visits at days 29/57/77, optional open-label extension to day 441. Primary: EDS (ESS for Children and Adolescents). Key secondaries: irritable/disruptive behaviors, hyperphagia (HQ-CT), ABC-2 domains, caregiver global impression. Status: recruiting; registry completion July 2027; company guides mid-2027 topline (timing previously pushed from H2 2026 on enrollment). ODD granted for PWS. FACT (CT.gov NCT06366464; company PRs)

Readout to date — Phase 2 signal-detection (n=65, 1:1:1 low/high dose/placebo, 11 weeks): ESS-CHAD improved −4.9 (high) / −4.1 (low) vs −3.7 placebo — not statistically significant; study was explicitly not powered for significance. Secondary signals favored the higher dose (ABC-2 improvements in ages 6–12, CaGI reductions ≥1.0, modest hyperphagia improvement). AEs: anxiety, headache, irritability. FACT (BioSpace; NeurologyLive; PatientWorthy) Honest read: the Ph2 was a trend, not a signal — TEMPO is a genuine binary on whether a larger, better-powered study separates from a −3.7-point placebo drift in a behaviorally noisy pediatric population. INTERPRETATION

4.4 ARGUS — EPX-100 in Dravet syndrome guidance

Phase 3, global, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, adjunctive therapy in children and adults with SCN1A-positive DS not adequately controlled on prior AEDs (≥4 countable motor seizures per 4-week baseline; stable AED regimen ≥30 days; fenfluramine prohibited). Primary endpoint: mean percent change in 28-day countable motor seizure frequency (CMS-28) across titration + maintenance. Secondaries: ≥50% responder rate, countable-motor-seizure-free days, Clinical Global Impression. FACT (NeurologyLive; company PRs) Readout to date — open-label extension (Dec 2025 AES): n=18 with ≥6 months exposure; median CMS-28 reduction ~50%; 50% achieved ≥50% reduction; generally well tolerated with up to ~3 years exposure; TEAEs >5%: seizures, pyrexia, URI; no significant GI AEs; no extra lab monitoring. 10 serious AEs in 5 patients (1 possibly related); no deaths; 1 discontinuation. FACT (company PR via BioSpace, Dec 8 2025) Topline guided H1 2027 (previously 2026); potential PDUFA 2028. FACT Statistical plan / powering / enrollment target: not located. GAP

4.5 LIGHTHOUSE — EPX-100 in Lennox-Gastaut syndrome guidance

Phase 3, global, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, adjunctive EPX-100 in LGS; enrolling; topline H1 2027. FACT Endpoint, enrollment target, and statistical plan: not located in sources reviewed. GAP Note the mechanistic stretch: clemizole's discovery model is SCN1A-specific (Dravet); LGS is etiologically heterogeneous, so the zebrafish-to-human translation argument is weaker here than in ARGUS. INTERPRETATION

4.6 Pitolisant GR (gastro-resistant tablets) — PDUFA April 1, 2027 hard

NDA accepted July 2026 for gastro-resistant (enteric-coated) pitolisant tablets, designed to lower the risk of GI side effects and let patients start at a therapeutic dose without titration; target action date April 1, 2027; planned launch H1 2027. FACT (Q2 2026 PR) It is not a pediatric formulation: WAKIX already carries a pediatric (6+) EDS approval (2024), and the six-month pediatric exclusivity is tied to completing TEMPO, not to GR. This is the nearest-term, lowest-volatility catalyst: a reformulation of an approved drug. Commercial relevance is lifecycle extension ahead of the 2030 cliff. INTERPRETATION

4.7 BP-205 — Phase 1 MAD (Q4 2026) → Phase 1b (early 2027) guidance

SAD complete (n=72): Tmax 30–75 min, t½ ~25 h, dose-proportional PK, no serious/severe TEAEs. Full MAD dataset expected Q4 2026; Phase 1b in sleep-deprived healthy volunteers (initiated Q3 2026) reads early 2027 — the first pharmacodynamic signal (wake-promotion under sleep deprivation); Phase 2 in multiple CNS indications planned mid-2027. FACT (Q2 2026 PR; 2027 catalyst dataset) These are sentiment events, not valuation binaries — but a clean MAD + positive sleep-deprivation PD would be the first human evidence the "most potent OX2R agonist" claim means anything clinically. 10 / RISKS

What could break the annuity

Ordered by potential impact on the investment case.

HIGH1 · Orexin-2 agonists obsolete the mechanism story in NT1

Orzeyful is approved and treats the underlying orexin deficiency; pitolisant is symptomatic histaminergic amplification. Prescriber and payer preference in NT1 can migrate over 2027–2029 regardless of WAKIX's execution. INTERPRETATION

HIGH2 · Single-product dependence into a 2030 cliff

WAKIX is 100% of revenue; contracted exclusivity ends March 2030 (generics licensed from 2030). The entire post-2030 equity story must be built by the 2027 readout cluster. FACT

HIGH3 · ONSTRIDE-2 repeats an already-failed experiment

INTUNE missed its IH primary in Oct 2023 (−33% stock). The high-dose rationale (60 mg succeeds where 35.6 mg failed) is unproven, and IH is not orexin-deficient. INTERPRETATION

HIGH4 · Two Phase 3 failures in two years

INTUNE (2023) and RECONNECT (2025) both missed primaries — the latter killing a $200M acquisition's lead asset. Late-stage execution credibility is dented even though both misses had plausible explanations (placebo response). FACT

MED5 · COGS creep on the only product

Cost of product sold rose to 24.2% of revenue (Novitium royalties) from 19.0% YoY — a structural margin step-up with Bioprojet tiered royalties underneath. FACT

MED6 · Capital allocation: Zynerba write-off

Up to $200M (Oct 2023) for ZYN002 → phased out after RECONNECT. Epygenix ($35M + milestones) is the next "prove it" deal. M&A has not yet created value. INTERPRETATION

MED7 · AET litigation tail risk

6 of 7 ANDA filers settled; AET's bench trial closes Oct 22, 2026. An adverse ruling could pull generic entry earlier than March 2030 on the polymorph/method-of-use patents. INTERPRETATION

MED8 · TEMPO's Phase 2 was a trend, not a signal

ESS-CHAD −4.9/−4.1 vs −3.7 placebo, not significant, in an underpowered n=65 study. The Ph3 (n=134) must separate from a large placebo drift in a behaviorally noisy pediatric population. INTERPRETATION

LOW9 · Officer selling into strength

CMO sold 33,954 shares (Sept 4/8, 2026) after option exercises; CEO sold via 10b5-1 at $40.00–40.28 (Dec 2025). Routine compensation harvesting, but no open-market insider buying located. FACT

LOW10 · Finance leadership in transition

Q2 2026 call featured an interim principal financial officer (Stephen Mollichella) alongside a newly granted CFO (Glenn Reicin, April 2026). Watch for a clean handoff. FACT

Three paths, priced with soft anchors

Framed around the clinical binaries, not commercial trading. Soft scenario price targets below are illustrative (explicitly labeled assumptions), not a recommendation. Takeout/max-value framing is capped at the street high of $70.

Bear case

ONSTRIDE-2 fails like INTUNE; TEMPO misses on placebo drift; ARGUS/LIGHTHOUSE miss on small-effect/noisy-endpoint dynamics; orexin agonists (Orzeyful now, alixorexton 2027) steadily take NT1 share; AET wins or narrows the patent case. WAKIX becomes a 3.4-year cash cow into a 2030 generic cliff with no replacement franchise. The profitable-multiple compresses toward a declining-annuity multiple. INTERPRETATION

Base case

Mixed 2027: pitolisant GR approved (Apr 2027); ONSTRIDE-1 hits (dose-response holds in narcolepsy, PDUFA 2028); one of TEMPO / ARGUS / LIGHTHOUSE hits, giving a second franchise; ONSTRIDE-2 is the miss. WAKIX grows through 2028–2029 on patient adds and the GR/HD lifecycle extensions, then faces 2030 generics with at least one new revenue leg. Valuation stays anchored to earnings with pipeline optionality priced in pieces as readouts land. INTERPRETATION

Bull case

Multiple 2027 hits: ONSTRIDE-1 + TEMPO + both EPX-100 trials, with clean safety. Two new franchises (PWS pitolisant, rare-epilepsy clemizole with ODD/RPDD and PRV-eligible pediatric designations) plus the HD lifecycle extension reframe Harmony from "WAKIX annuity" to a multi-franchise CNS company; BP-205 Ph1b/Ph2 data keeps an orexin call option alive. The 2030 cliff is bridged by new exclusivity stacks (new formulations, new indications) — though a new formulation or indication earns 3-year US exclusivity for that change only and does not keep generic IR pitolisant out, and pediatric exclusivity is already counted in the March 2030 date. Takeout or max-value scenarios under this path are bounded above by the street high of $70. INTERPRETATION
