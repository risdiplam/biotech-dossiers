01 / FRAME

A real hit, a thin margin, and a valuation that was never really about mania alone

Rapport Therapeutics (Nasdaq: RAPP; CIK 0002012593; Delaware; HQ Boston, MA) is a clinical-stage precision-neuroscience company built on receptor-associated protein (RAP) biology. Its lead asset, RAP-219 — a TARPγ8-selective AMPA-receptor negative allosteric modulator in-licensed from Janssen (as JNJ-64300912) — is simultaneously in Phase 3 for focal onset seizures (FOS), freshly Phase 2-positive in bipolar mania, and headed for Phase 3 in primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures (PGTCS). On October 20, 2026 the mania trial met its primary and secondary endpoints. The stock's premarket spike into the $50s fully round-tripped within the session. This dossier adjudicates the dispute with the disclosed numbers.

What the "it hit" camp has right

  • The primary endpoint was prespecified and met (p=0.045), and the prespecified secondary (CGI-BP mania severity, −0.4, p=0.024) hit too — two aligned wins, not one cherry-picked scale.
  • All 11 YMRS items moved in the drug's favor; the four nominally significant items (irritability, thought content, disruptive-aggressive behavior, sexual interest) are core mania symptoms, not filler items.
  • Separation was still widening at Week 3 (−0.9 → −2.2 → −3.1), at a single 0.75 mg dose that sits below the 1.25 mg regimen used in epilepsy — with no akathisia, no EPS, no orthostatic hypotension reported and somnolence below placebo (0.8% vs 3.1%).
  • Stifel — the most quoted skeptic on the day — still called it a "clear positive" against low expectations and said safety "actually looks pretty good." H.C. Wainwright raised its target to $66 from $40 after the print.

What the "mediocre" camp has right

  • p=0.045 is a near-boundary result in a trial where 24.6% of the drug arm discontinued treatment (vs 11.8% on placebo) and the estimate rests on a missing-at-random model — sensitivity analyses are not yet public.
  • The placebo arm improved −12.3 points (a ~34% drop from baseline), and the drug's 3.1-point delta is at the low end of the historical antimanic range (§05) — below the ~4.9-point pooled monotherapy average in the asenapine-era meta-analysis.
  • The company disclosed no responder (≥50% YMRS reduction) or remission rates — the numbers clinicians usually anchor on, and the numbers sponsors usually lead with when they are strong.
  • All-cause discontinuation ran 2:1 against the drug, driven by consent withdrawal (16.7% vs 4.7%). In a 21-day inpatient trial, patients voting with their feet is itself tolerability-relevant information, whatever the AE table says.

INTERPRETATION — dossier verdict: The readout is best read as proof of mechanism at a probably submaximal dose, not proof of a competitive product. It converts mania from a free option into a real, financeable Phase 3 program, and it validates TARPγ8 pharmacology in a second, placebo-controlled setting — which also de-risks the epilepsy thesis modestly. It does not yet show an antimanic effect that would win share from $0.10 generic atypicals on efficacy; the commercial case would have to be won on tolerability and adherence. Whether the October 20 price (~$972M EV) over- or under-pays for that package depends mostly on FOS Phase 3 — see §09 scenarios.

03 / SCIENCE

TARPγ8: selling anatomical precision in a pathway famous for blunt instruments

Plain version first, then the technical case — and the specific ways the precision story can fail.

The plain-English version

Glutamate is the brain's main "go" signal, and most fast go-signaling runs through one receptor family: AMPA receptors. Drugs that turn down AMPA signaling work — perampanel (Fycompa), a broad AMPA blocker, is an approved epilepsy drug — but because AMPA receptors are everywhere, turning them down everywhere causes dizziness, unsteadiness, sedation and behavioral effects. RAP-219 tries to turn down AMPA signaling only where a specific helper protein, TARPγ8, is present. TARPγ8 lives mostly in the hippocampus, neocortex and amygdala — regions where focal seizures start and that sit inside the mood circuits implicated in mania — and is largely absent from the hindbrain, where many dose-limiting effects are generated. Same pathway, narrower address.

The technical case

  • FACT RAP-219 is a selective negative allosteric modulator (NAM) of TARPγ8-containing AMPA receptors. TARPs (transmembrane AMPAR regulatory proteins) control AMPAR trafficking, localization and gating; γ8 is the dominant TARP in hippocampal/neocortical principal neurons. The molecule originated at Janssen as JNJ-64300912 and was licensed to Rapport in August 2022; Rapport's scientific founder David Bredt (ex-Global Head of Neuroscience Discovery, Janssen) pioneered RAP targeting there. [R5][R13]
  • FACT Human PET work with a companion radiotracer confirmed γ8 distribution and receptor occupancy (RO): the 10-K cites predicted therapeutic exposure of ~2 ng/mL (≈50% RO) to ~3.5 ng/mL (≈70% RO), with once-daily dosing able to meet/exceed those levels and low CYP-mediated drug-interaction risk in Phase 1. A Phase 1 PET abstract (AES) dosed healthy volunteers at 0.25–1.25 mg QD in RO cohorts. [R5][R14]
  • FACT Preclinical proof: Matta et al. (Epilepsia, 2026) showed selective TARPγ8 NAMs (RAP-219 and analog RTX-1738) produced dose-dependent antiseizure effects in corneal-kindling and PTZ models, maintained over 7 days of dosing, at exposures that did not impair motor function (rotarod) — the preclinical basis for a wide therapeutic index claim. [R15]
  • FACT The mania rationale is glutamatergic: manic states show elevated cortical glutamate/glutamine and cortico-limbic hypermetabolism (Brooks 2010; Yüksel & Öngür 2010; Gigante 2012, as cited in the company's Oct 20 deck), and lithium, valproate and lamotrigine each attenuate glutamatergic transmission in part. No dedicated antimanic drug has been developed against this biology before; every approved acute-mania agent works primarily through dopamine D2 blockade or ion-channel/mood-stabilizer pharmacology. [R16]

Why it might fail

  • INTERPRETATION γ8 may be the right address for seizures and only an approximate one for mania. Mania circuitry (ventral striatum, prefrontal-limbic loops) is broader than the hippocampal/neocortical γ8 map. A partial-circuit drug can produce a real but capped effect — which is one coherent reading of a 3.1-point delta that was still rising at Week 3.
  • INTERPRETATION Exposure-response is unproven in mania. The mania trial tested one maintenance dose (0.75 mg) — below the 1.25 mg epilepsy maintenance dose — with two titration speeds pooled. There is no mania dose-response curve. The company's Phase 3 fix (1.25 mg, modeled to reach RO thresholds faster and higher by Day 21) is a hypothesis, not a result.
  • FACT The class carries a behavioral shadow. Perampanel's US label carries a boxed warning for serious psychiatric and behavioral reactions (including aggression/hostility), and broad AMPAR antagonism is associated with dizziness/ataxia. In the mania trial, agitation was the most common TEAE on RAP-219 (16.7% vs 9.4%) — mostly week 1, median 2 days, investigator-judged unrelated in >80% — but in a mania population an activation-type signal is exactly what a Phase 3 (and an FDA reviewer) will interrogate hardest. [R1][R17]
  • INTERPRETATION Precision cuts both ways commercially. If efficacy tops out a point below the best D2 agents, the product must win on tolerability/adherence — a claim that requires long-term, real-world-style data Rapport will not have for years, in a market where the incumbents are cheap generics.

Phase 2a focal epilepsy (the first proof) — read with its design caveat attached

  • FACT Results over the 8-week treatment period (10-K): ≥30% LE reduction in 85.2% (p<0.0001 vs baseline); median LE reduction 71.0% (p=0.0001); ≥50% clinical-seizure responder rate 72.0% (mITT-CS N=25; p<0.0001); seizure freedom 24.0%; median clinical-seizure reduction 77.8% (p=0.01). 26/30 (86.7%) completed dosing; 3 (10.0%) discontinued for AEs. [R5]
  • INTERPRETATION These are large numbers against a hard endpoint (device-recorded electrographic long episodes), in genuinely drug-resistant patients, on stable background ASMs. But the trial is single-arm against baseline — no placebo group — in 30 highly selected RNS-implanted patients. Regression to the mean, expectancy and diary effects cannot be excluded by design; that is precisely what FOCUS 1/2 (placebo-controlled, n≈645 combined) exist to resolve. FDA's Dec 2025 end-of-Phase 2 feedback supported advancing to the two pivotals. [R5]

Regulatory state

  • FACT FOS: FDA end-of-Phase 2 feedback (Dec 2025) supported two Phase 3 trials; both are enrolling. Mania: the Phase 2 was executed under an IND with a modified statistical analysis plan and increased enrollment during the trial — the Q2 2026 10-Q states this was done so the trial could "potentially be considered as confirmatory evidence of effectiveness"; the company nonetheless plans an end-of-Phase 2 meeting in 1Q 2027 and two Phase 3 mania trials from 2H 2027. Whether FDA treats the Phase 2 as supportive-only or as part of the confirmatory package is an open, value-relevant question for that meeting. [R4][R1]
  • FACT China: Tenacia holds exclusive RAP-219 rights for mainland China/Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan (Mar 2026 license; §10) and is obligated to run the territory portion of global FOS and mania trials. [R4]

The benchmark the market used (cross-trial, with caveats — not head-to-head)

  • FACT Yildiz et al.'s meta-analysis of 38 acute-mania RCTs (10,800 patients): pooled antimanic effect Hedges g = 0.42 (95% CI 0.36–0.48); most approved agents sit at g ≈ 0.26–0.46; pooled responder rates 48% drug vs 31% placebo (NNT ≈ 6). [R18]
  • FACT In the asenapine meta-analysis, the pooled placebo-adjusted YMRS improvement for atypical-antipsychotic monotherapy comparators was ~4.9 points (95% CI 3.8–6.0); asenapine itself ~4.5. Cochrane's aripiprazole analysis: −3.66 points vs placebo at 3 weeks. Stifel's Oct 20 framing — "the range that's considered competitive with available drugs today (3–4 pts)" — matches this literature. [R19][R20]
  • INTERPRETATION RAP-219's 3.1 points, converted at a typical mania-trial change-score SD of ~10–11, is a standardized effect of ~0.28–0.31 — the low end of the approved-agent distribution, below its pooled mean. Caveats that cut the other way: RAP-219's placebo arm improved an unusually large −12.3 points (inpatient milieu + rescue lorazepam + a 36-point baseline), which compresses any delta; cross-trial comparisons mix eras, baselines (~36 here is severe), designs and rescue rules, and are not evidence of inferiority in a head-to-head sense. They are, however, the comparison set prescribers and payers will use.

The adjudication

INTERPRETATION "It hit" is true and "mediocre" is also defensible — they are answers to different questions. As a proof-of-concept experiment, the trial did its job: a novel, non-dopaminergic mechanism produced a statistically significant, internally consistent (primary + secondary + all 11 items + still-widening trajectory) antimanic effect with a tolerability profile no D2 agent can match on paper. As a product preview, it showed the low end of the competitive efficacy range, at a sub-epilepsy dose, with a fragile p-value, a 2:1 all-cause discontinuation imbalance the AE table doesn't capture, and no responder analysis disclosed. The Phase 3 program the company outlined on the day — 1.25 mg dosing to reach receptor-occupancy thresholds faster, ex-US sites with historically cleaner neuropsych signal, protocol optimization — is a coherent attempt to widen the delta, and an implicit concession that 3.1 points at 0.75 mg is not the intended final number. What Phase 3 must show to settle the argument: (1) a placebo-adjusted YMRS delta ≥4 points at the higher dose with p comfortably below 0.05 in at least one of two trials; (2) disclosed responder/remission rates in line with the ~48%/31% historical frame; (3) all-cause discontinuation at parity with placebo; (4) the agitation signal resolved as dose/titration-manageable rather than mechanistic. Until then, mania is a credible option, not a priced asset.

06 / LANDSCAPE

Differentiation is a tolerability argument in mania, an efficacy argument in epilepsy

RAP-219 will not be sold against placebo. In mania it faces generic dopamine blockers that work fast and cost little, with liabilities patients hate. In focal epilepsy it faces a crowded ASM shelf — including the one approved drug that shares its pathway.

Acute bipolar mania

  • FACT Approved antimanic agents (atypical antipsychotics — olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone, aripiprazole, ziprasidone, asenapine, cariprazine; plus lithium, valproate, carbamazepine) deliver pooled placebo-adjusted YMRS effects in the ~3–5-point band (§05). Their class liabilities are the differentiation surface: EPS/akathisia (most D2 agents; aripiprazole akathisia is label-prominent), weight gain and metabolic effects (olanzapine, quetiapine worst-in-class), sedation/somnolence, prolactin elevation (risperidone/paliperidone), QT effects (ziprasidone), and for lithium/valproate/carbamazepine: blood-level monitoring, renal/thyroid toxicity, teratogenicity and drug interactions. [R18][R19][R20]
  • INTERPRETATION RAP-219's Week-3 snapshot — no EPS/akathisia/orthostasis, somnolence below placebo, no reported weight/metabolic signal, no monitoring, once-daily oral — is exactly the profile that could win maintenance-adjacent acute use and psychiatrist mindshare, if Phase 3 efficacy lands ≥4 points. At 3.1 points with generic risperidone/olanzapine available, the tolerability case alone is a hard sell to payers. Note also what RAP-219 is not competing with in mania: xanomeline-trospium (Cobenfy) and lumateperone (Caplyta) are not approved for acute mania; the "first new mechanism in decades" framing is about mania specifically and is fair on the approved-label record.

Focal onset seizures

  • FACT Perampanel (Fycompa) — the approved non-selective AMPAR NAM — sets the pathway benchmark. In its pivotal adjunctive FOS trials, median seizure-frequency change was −23.3% (4 mg) and −30.8% (8 mg) vs −10.7% placebo (Study 306), with 50% responder rates of 28.5%/34.9% vs 17.9%; pooled 8 mg: −28.8% median change, 35.3% responders vs −12.8%/19.3% placebo. Its label carries a boxed warning for serious psychiatric/behavioral reactions, and dizziness/somnolence/ataxia drive discontinuation (long-term extension withdrawal for TEAEs ~20%). This is the tolerability bar RAP-219's γ8 selectivity is designed to clear — FOCUS 1/2 use the same registrational yardstick (median % change at Week 14; 50% responder secondary). [R17][R9][R10]
  • FACT The wider FOS shelf includes cenobamate (high seizure-freedom rates in drug-resistant patients, DRESS warning and slow titration), brivaracetam/levetiracetam (behavioral AEs), lacosamide, eslicarbazepine, and a deep generic base — plus device therapy (RNS, DBS, VNS) in the drug-resistant segment Rapport targets first. Differentiation claimed for RAP-219: once-daily low-milligram dosing, no titration burden of cenobamate's kind, low DDI risk in a polypharmacy population, and — if the Ph2a signal is real at all — effect sizes above the perampanel band. None of that is established against a placebo yet.

PGTCS

FACT Perampanel is also approved for PGTCS (adjunctive), as are lamotrigine, levetiracetam and topiramate; the segment is smaller than FOS but less crowded at the novel-mechanism end. Rapport's Phase 3 start (guided 1H 2027) extends the same molecule into generalized epilepsy — a label-breadth play that matters for ASM adoption, since clinicians prefer agents covering both seizure types.

12 / RISKS

Ranked red flags

Ordered by expected value impact, not by order of appearance.

  1. The value engine is uncontrolled data. FOS — the indication carrying most of the ~$972M EV — has never beaten placebo. The Ph2a was single-arm, n=30, in device-implanted patients. If FOCUS 1/2 shrink the effect toward the perampanel band (or below), the equity re-rates toward cash-plus-mania-option in one print, and that print is years away (registry: 2029), maximizing duration risk.
  2. Mania effect size is at the competitive floor. 3.1 points (g≈0.3) vs a ~3–4-point "competitive" band and ~4.9-point pooled atypical average. The entire mania thesis depends on the 1.25 mg Phase 3 dose producing a bigger number — an unproven exposure-response assumption in this population.
  3. p=0.045 fragility + missing-data structure. A near-boundary p-value with 24.6% drug-arm discontinuation under an MAR model. If FDA or a journal reviewer demands sensitivity analyses that move the estimate, the "hit" narrative weakens retroactively. The undisclosed responder/remission rates are a conspicuous omission until proven innocent.
  4. Discontinuation asymmetry. 2:1 all-cause discontinuation against the drug, consent-withdrawal-led, in an inpatient setting — the single most under-discussed number in the release, and a direct challenge to the company's adherence-based commercial story. Phase 3 must close this gap, not just the YMRS gap.
  5. Agitation as a potential mechanistic signal. 16.7% vs 9.4% in a class (AMPAR antagonism) whose approved member carries a boxed warning for psychiatric/behavioral reactions. Mostly mild, week-1, investigator-dismissed — but Wells Fargo's post-print note explicitly flagged "questions on safety," and any dose increase to 1.25 mg could amplify an activation signal.
  6. Five pivotal-scale trials, one balance sheet. FOCUS 1/2 running; PGTCS Ph3 (1H 2027) and two mania Ph3s (2H 2027) guided. Runway "into 2H 2029" assumes the current slate costs what management thinks; slippage anywhere creates a financing overhang directly on top of the FOS readout window.
  7. Royalty stack and license dependency. RAP-219 exists by license from Janssen, with up to ~$116M+ in milestones and mid-to-high-single-digit worldwide royalties owed on the lead candidate — a permanent margin tax and a counterparty dependency (including diligence obligations) embedded in the core asset.
  8. Single-asset concentration. Every clinical program is RAP-219. A class-level safety finding (e.g., in the long-term OLE) hits epilepsy, mania and PGTCS simultaneously; there is no second clinical asset to rotate into. RAP-641 is pre-Phase 1.
  9. Thin, momentum-driven tape. ~0.3M shares/day of baseline liquidity, a −31% slide from the Sep 22 close into the readout, and an 8.2M-share readout day: the stock's price discovery is flow-dominated, and the $110M ATM gives management a standing incentive to sell into strength — capping reflexive upside after good news.
  10. Insider optics. Pre-planned but persistent insider selling through 2026 (CEO included) with no offsetting open-market buys found. Benign in structure, negative in signal — and it will be re-litigated by the market at every future drawdown.
