2 · Science: Mechanism of Action & Why It Could Fail

OV329 — next-gen GABA-AT inhibitor (epilepsy)

FACT GABA is the brain's major inhibitory neurotransmitter; GABA-aminotransferase (GABA-AT) is the key enzyme that degrades it. OV329 substantially reduces GABA-AT activity, raising synaptic and extrasynaptic GABA and boosting phasic + tonic inhibitory neurotransmission, which curbs excessive neuronal firing. Source: Ovid corporate decks (2024–2025); Silverman RB, Chem Rev 2018;118:4037–4070 (GABA-AT inactivation review, PMID 29569907).

FACT OV329 was designed in the Richard Silverman laboratory (Northwestern University); Ovid holds license agreements with Northwestern University. Preclinical anticonvulsant activity was published: Feja et al., Epilepsia 2021;62:3091–3104 (pentylenetetrazole seizure-threshold test and amygdala-kindled rats). The mechanism-based inactivator chemistry was described in Juncosa et al., J Am Chem Soc 2018;140:2151–2164. Source: company decks; Northwestern seminar flyer; AES 2023 poster.

FACT Company claim: OV329 is ~1,000-fold more potent than vigabatrin at GABA-AT (IC50 ~0.1–0.3 µM vs ~60–100 mM range) with a different enzyme-modification chemistry (described as electrostatic/irreversible vs vigabatrin's covalent enamine pathway), enabling low mg-range daily dosing and, critically, no retinal accumulation: preclinical head-to-head showed OV329 tissue exposure below quantification in retina while vigabatrin showed ~4× accumulation. Source: Aug 2025 corporate deck (company data on file).

OV4071 / OV350 — direct KCC2 activators (psychosis, epilepsy)

FACT KCC2 (potassium-chloride cotransporter 2) is a neuron-specific chloride extruder that sets the chloride gradient enabling GABA to be inhibitory; reduced KCC2 activity produces excitatory/inhibitory imbalance implicated in epilepsy, schizophrenia, and other hyperexcitability disorders. Ovid's compounds bind and directly activate KCC2. The portfolio was in-licensed from AstraZeneca. Source: 2023 corporate deck; Aug 13, 2026 PR; Duy, He, He & Kahle, Expert Opin Ther Targets 2020;24:629–637 (KCC2 therapeutic-targeting review).

FACT The discovery/preclinical characterization of the IV compound OV350 was published: Jarvis et al., Cell Reports Medicine 2023;4:100957 — rapid brain accumulation after IV/IP dosing, a translatable EEG high-gamma pharmacodynamic biomarker, and restoration of diazepam efficacy in a refractory status-epilepticus model. In psychosis models, OV350/OV4071 suppressed MK-801-induced hyperlocomotion (company poster, APA 2026; authors Toshiya Nishi et al., Ovid) with activity described as comparable to or greater than clozapine, without sedation up to 75 mg/kg. Source: 2023 deck citing Jarvis 2023; APA 2026 poster P08-012.

FACT First-in-human data for IV OV350 (reported Dec 2025): no treatment-related serious adverse events; PK as predicted; exploratory electrophysiology suggested GABAergic CNS activity consistent with the mechanism — described by the company as the first-ever clinical validation of direct KCC2 activation in humans. OV4071 is the first oral direct KCC2 activator, now in Phase 1 in healthy volunteers (Australia, HREC + TGA clinical-trial notification). Source: Mar 18, 2026 PR; Aug 13, 2026 PR (company claims).

OV888 (GV101) — selective ROCK2 inhibitor (CCM) — PAUSED

FACT OV888/GV101 is a potent, highly selective ROCK2 inhibitor (company-claimed ~1,071-fold ROCK1/ROCK2 selectivity) licensed from Graviton Bioscience (Apr 2023 agreement; $10M Graviton preferred-stock purchase; tiered mid- to high-teens royalties if commercialized; territory excludes ALS and China/HK/Macau/Taiwan). ROCK2 regulates actin-cytoskeletal signaling relevant to endothelial dysfunction in cerebral cavernous malformations (CCM). A Phase 1 MAD study (100–600 mg QD × 7 days, healthy volunteers) showed dose-dependent IL-17/IL-21 suppression as a PD marker of ROCK2 inhibition, with headache the most common AE (23%, transient). Source: Q2 2026 10-Q Note 11; Jul 1, 2024 topline PR; 2024 corporate deck.

FACT The program is currently paused (Q2 2026 10-Q: "The program related to this collaboration agreement is currently paused"); program expense for OV888 was $0 in H1 2026 and negative in 2025 (settlement/contra-expense with the collaboration partner). It contributes no near-term catalyst. Source: Q2 2026 10-Q Notes 11 & 14.

Why each program could fail

OV329 (GABA-AT) — the vigabatrin shadow

  • FACT Vigabatrin, the only approved GABA-AT inhibitor, causes irreversible bilateral concentric visual-field constriction in ~40–50% of adult patients (15–31% in infants), typically asymptomatic until severe, slowly progressive and irreversible; retinal vigabatrin concentrations run 5–18× brain levels, raising retinal GABA ~5× and triggering excitotoxicity. Risk factors: cumulative dose and treatment duration. Source: IOVS (Wild et al.) retinal nerve-fiber imaging study; Karger Cell Physiol Biochem 40:831 (vigabatrin retinal toxicity/photopic/taurine study); Springer pediatric VFD review (prevalence 15–50%).
  • INTERPRETATION OV329's whole differentiation thesis is "vigabatrin's efficacy without vigabatrin's retina." The supporting evidence is preclinical (no retinal accumulation in animals; therapeutic-index proxy in Sprague-Dawley rats) plus short-duration Phase 1 ophthalmic testing (BCVA, fundus photography, OCT, automated perimetry through Day 30, 21–23 days post–last dose — no changes). That is reassuring but not dispositive: vigabatrin's toxicity is cumulative over months–years, and an 8-week Phase 2a + open-label extension is the first real test. Any ocular signal in Phase 2 would be thesis-breaking.
  • INTERPRETATION Raising brain GABA broadly is not free: sedation, cognitive dulling, and behavioral effects are the classic GABAergic tax (vigabatrin itself carries behavioral/psychiatric AEs). The Phase 2a must show that low-mg dosing threads the needle between seizure reduction and tolerability in patients already on 0–3 concomitant anti-seizure meds.
  • GAP No published head-to-head clinical data of OV329 vs vigabatrin; the "best-in-category safety" claim rests on company preclinical work and short Phase 1 follow-up.

OV4071/OV350 (KCC2) — novel human biology, no approved-drug validation

  • FACT No drug acting via direct KCC2 activation has ever been approved; the human pharmacology is first-in-class. Ovid states its molecules are the "only known direct KCC2 activators." UNVERIFIED Independent verification: this dossier's searches surfaced no other clinical-stage direct KCC2 activator; the claim remains a company assertion. Source: 2023 corporate deck; independent web/literature search Oct 2026.
  • INTERPRETATION First-in-class CNS mechanisms have a long failure record when EEG/biomarker signals fail to convert into clinical efficacy. OV350's human data are exploratory electrophysiology in healthy volunteers; OV4071's 2027 schizophrenia PoC will be the first patient efficacy test of the mechanism. The ketamine-challenge study (H2 2026) is a translational proxy, not a substitute.
  • INTERPRETATION The lead indication has already pivoted once: March 2026 PRs framed OV4071 around psychosis in Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia; by August 2026 the Phase 2 PoC is targeted at acute schizophrenia. Acute schizophrenia enrolls faster and reads out sooner, but it is a far more competitive indication (see §6) and a larger efficacy bar than PDP, where only one drug is approved.
  • GAP Phase 1 design details (sample size, SAD/MAD dose levels, PD endpoints beyond safety/PK) have not been disclosed; the magnitude of any KCC2-engagement signal in humans is unknown.

Cross-cutting scientific risk

  • FACT The company's own 10-K/10-Q risk factors state the canonical warning: preclinical and early-clinical success is not predictive of later-trial outcomes, citing soticlestat — which succeeded in Phase 1b/2a and Phase 2 (ELEKTRA) yet failed both Phase 3 trials (SKYLINE in Dravet, SKYWAY in LGS) in June 2024 and was discontinued by Takeda in January 2025. Source: Q2 2026 10-Q Risk Factors; 2025 10-K.

4 · Trial Design Deep Dive

OV329 Phase 2a in treatment-resistant focal onset seizures — the defining binary

  • FACT Design (per Oct 2025 company deck): multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, 2:1 randomization to OV329 vs placebo; population 18–55 years, diagnosed treatment-refractory focal aware or focal impaired-awareness motor-onset seizures, on 0–3 concomitant anti-seizure medications stable ≥1 month. Schedule: ~1-week screening, ~1-week baseline, 8-week treatment, follow-up/taper. Dose: 7 mg. Planned enrollment N = 240–360, with an open-label extension. Source: Ovid Oct 2025 Phase 1 topline deck (Quartr).
  • FACT Endpoints: safety (including ophthalmic, neurological, neurophysiology exams), pharmacokinetics; efficacy: percent change from baseline in monthly seizure frequency, responder rate (≥50% reduction), time to return to pre-randomization baseline seizure frequency, seizure-free days, Clinical Global Impression of Improvement (CGI-I). The company describes the study as designed with "registrational-level rigor to potentially support multiple future seizure indications." Source: Oct 2025 deck; Aug 13, 2026 PR.
  • GAP No NCT number located in this dossier's research; statistical plan, powering assumptions, interim-analysis provisions, and the exact primary-endpoint hierarchy have not been publicly disclosed. The deck's N=240–360 range (vs. the n=30/15 figures on the same slide, which appear to describe a sub-cohort) leaves the actual powered sample size unclear — a material unknown for judging the readout's informativeness.
  • FACT Context on the patient population: treatment-resistant focal epilepsy trials typically enroll patients with high baseline seizure burden (for reference, Xenon's X-TOLE2 Phase 3 enrolled n=380, median 5 prior ASMs, baseline 12.75 seizures/month). Ovid has not disclosed baseline-seizure entry criteria. Source: Xenon X-TOLE2 PR via trade press, Mar 2026 — comparator context only, not a head-to-head.

OV329 photosensitivity proof-of-mechanism study — the year-end 2026 read

  • FACT Open-label photostimulation (photosensitivity/PPR) study in patients with photosensitive epilepsy; testing three doses (5, 7, 9 mg) for anti-convulsant effect on the photoparoxysmal response; regulatory clearances received; study initiated (Netherlands specialized site); results guided near year-end 2026. Source: Aug 13, 2026 PR; q4-2026-dossier dataset (guidance quality).
  • INTERPRETATION The photosensitivity model is a well-established early human signal for anti-seizure activity — intermittent photic stimulation provokes a measurable EEG response that known anti-seizure drugs suppress — making it a legitimate, low-cost proof-of-mechanism gate before the Phase 2a reads out. But it is an acute challenge model: a positive PPR read supports pharmacology, it does not predict the magnitude of chronic seizure-frequency reduction in refractory FOS, and false reads in either direction are possible.

OV329 TSC-associated seizures PoC (Q4 2026 initiation) & infantile spasms (2027)

  • FACT Proof-of-concept safety and signal-finding study in TSC-associated seizures to initiate Q4 2026 using a weight-based pediatric formulation (independent from the adult fixed-dose tablet); to inform dosing for a subsequent infantile-spasms study anticipated in 2027. Company notes GABA-AT inhibition is an established-effective mechanism in these developmental epileptic encephalopathies (i.e., vigabatrin's approved/used indications). Source: Aug 13, 2026 PR.
  • FACT Preclinical support: an AES abstract (Ovid-funded; NIH NINDS grants NS108378/NS111064/NS111338) showed OV329 reduced NMDA-induced flexion-seizure severity and count in a mouse infantile-spasm model, increased tonic (not phasic) inhibitory current in hippocampal neurons, and outperformed ACTH on motor-coordination measures. Source: AES abstracts listing (cms.aesnet.org).
  • GAP No disclosed design, sample size, endpoints, or readout timing for the TSC PoC or the IS study.

OV329 Phase 1 readouts to date (healthy volunteers)

  • FACT Oct 2025 topline (SAD/MAD): strong GABA-AT inhibitory activity ("53% GABA inhibition" per trade-press headline of the PR), no treatment-related ocular findings across extensive ophthalmic testing (BCVA, fundus photography, dilated ophthalmoscopy, OCT, automated perimetry) through Day 30 (21–23 days post–last dose); potentially treatment-related AEs were mild (headache, drowsiness at 2 mg; metallic taste at 5 mg). A 7 mg cohort was subsequently completed with "favorable safety and tolerability" (Mar 2026 PR). Source: Oct 3, 2025 PR; Mar 18, 2026 PR; AES 2025 late-breaking poster (Tsai, Rotenberg, Rogasch et al., abstract 2.451).
  • FACT Target-engagement biomarkers (AES 2025 poster): cortical silent period prolonged vs placebo (FDI +12.9 ms, p=0.00012; APB +7.88 ms, p=0.026); medial parietal GABA +7.13% vs +0.24% placebo at 5 mg after 7 days (not statistically significant given baseline variability); exploratory EEG shifts consistent with increased inhibition. Source: NeurologyLive summary of AES 2025 poster; Ovid AES2025 poster PDF.
  • INTERPRETATION Phase 1 established tolerability and target engagement in healthy volunteers — the necessary but not sufficient conditions. None of these endpoints measure seizures in patients.

OV4071 Phase 1 + ketamine-challenge proof-of-mechanism

  • FACT Phase 1 in healthy volunteers ongoing (Australia; HREC approval + TGA clinical-trial notification received Q1 2026); objectives: safety, tolerability, PK/PD of the first oral direct KCC2 activator. Data intended to support a Phase 2 proof-of-concept in acute schizophrenia in 2027. Source: Mar 18, 2026 PR; Aug 13, 2026 PR.
  • FACT Ketamine-challenge study to initiate H2 2026: in healthy volunteers, designed to reproduce glutamate/dopamine surges and assess electrophysiology/biomarkers relevant to schizophrenia — a pharmacodynamic proof-of-mechanism step. Source: Aug 13, 2026 PR.
  • INTERPRETATION The ketamine model is a standard translational psychiatry tool, but it models acute glutamatergic dysregulation, not the clinical syndrome of schizophrenia; a clean biomarker read de-risks pharmacology, not patient efficacy. The pivot from PDP/LBD (Mar 2026) to acute schizophrenia (Aug 2026) as the first PoC indication trades a thinly-treated niche for a faster-enrolling but far more competitive one.
  • GAP Phase 2 PoC design (population, endpoints — e.g., PANSS vs SAPS-PD — sample size, duration) undisclosed; initiation timing within 2027 unspecified.

  • INTERPRETATION Regulatory outlook: OV329's FOS Phase 2a is explicitly designed with "registrational-level rigor," suggesting the company hopes a single strong Phase 2a could anchor a registrational program — but there is no disclosed SPA, no FDA alignment disclosed, and focal-epilepsy registrational precedent (e.g., X-TOLE2) required two adequate, well-controlled trials' worth of evidence for NDA. Treat "registrational-level rigor" as design aspiration, not regulatory agreement.
  • GAP No disclosed FDA interactions (pre-IND, EOP1, SPA), no disclosed primary-endpoint agreement, and no disclosed pediatric-study-plan obligations for the DEE programs.

6 · Competition — Clinical Differentiation Only

Refractory focal onset seizures (OV329)

  • FACT Same-mechanism incumbent: vigabatrin (Sabril, Lundbeck) — irreversible GABA-AT inhibitor, FDA-approved as adjunctive therapy for refractory complex partial seizures in adults and as monotherapy for infantile spasms; use is constrained by the boxed warning for irreversible bilateral visual-field loss and a REMS program. OV329's clinical differentiation thesis vs vigabatrin is entirely safety/PK-based (no retinal accumulation, lower mg dosing, rapid brain clearance) — not a claimed efficacy advantage. Sabril US label (boxed warning/REMS); Ovid decks.
  • FACT New-mechanism near-term entrant: azetukalner (XEN1101, Xenon Pharmaceuticals) — Kv7.2/7.3 potassium-channel opener; Phase 3 X-TOLE2 positive (25 mg: −53.2% median monthly seizure reduction vs −10.4% placebo, p≈6×10⁻¹²; RR50 54.8% vs 20.8%; n=380, median 5 prior ASMs); NDA submission planned Q3 2026. If approved, it would reach the refractory FOS market well before OV329. Xenon X-TOLE2 PR, Mar 2026 (trade-press summaries); touchNEUROLOGY AAN 2026 review. Comparator context only — no cross-trial efficacy claim vs OV329 is made.
  • FACT Approved refractory-FOS armamentarium includes cenobamate (Xcopri, SK Life Science — high responder rates in refractory patients), brivaracetam, lacosamide, perampanel, and others — a crowded adjunctive market where new entrants differentiate on efficacy magnitude, tolerability, and dosing convenience. Established labels; no head-to-head claim made.
  • INTERPRETATION OV329's commercial/clinical bar is therefore twofold: (a) prove it is meaningfully safer than vigabatrin on chronic dosing, and (b) show seizure-reduction efficacy competitive with modern adjunctive agents in a population that will increasingly have azetukalner available. The Phase 2a placebo-controlled design answers (b) vs placebo, not vs active comparators.

TSC-associated seizures & infantile spasms (OV329 DEE expansion)

  • FACT TSC-associated seizures: approved/used options include everolimus (Afinitor — EXIST-3 Phase 3 in TSC partial-onset seizures), cannabidiol (Epidiolex — approved for TSC seizures 2020, GWPCARE6), and vigabatrin (particularly for TSC-associated infantile spasms, where it is first-line). IS standard of care: ACTH, high-dose prednisolone, vigabatrin. Drug labels; EXIST-3 (Franz et al., Lancet 2016); GWPCARE6 (Thiele et al.).
  • INTERPRETATION The DEE expansion is strategically coherent — GABA-AT inhibition is already validated in exactly these populations by vigabatrin — but it also means OV329 would launch into indications where vigabatrin's risk/benefit is already accepted by prescribers for short-course IS therapy, narrowing the differentiation to chronic-use settings (TSC maintenance) and prescriber comfort.

Psychosis / schizophrenia (OV4071)

  • FACT PDP psychosis: pimavanserin (Nuplazid, Acadia) is the only FDA-approved drug for hallucinations/delusions associated with Parkinson's disease psychosis (approved Apr 2016) — a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist with no D2 blockade (hence no motor worsening); pivotal trial: SAPS-PD −5.79 vs −2.73 placebo (n=199, 6 weeks); carries QT prolongation and the class boxed warning on mortality in elderly dementia-related psychosis. Low-dose quetiapine/clozapine are used off-label. Nuplazid label; pivotal trial summary (Cummings et al., Lancet 2014).
  • FACT Acute schizophrenia: Cobenfy (KarXT, xanomeline-trospium, BMS) — muscarinic agonist — was FDA-approved Sept 2024, the first new schizophrenia mechanism in decades; by contrast, AbbVie's emraclidine (M4 PAM) failed Phase 2 (EMPOWER-1/2, late 2024) and Sumitomo's ulotaront (TAAR1) failed Phase 3 (2023). FDA approvals; company PRs 2023–2024.
  • INTERPRETATION The lesson for OV4071: novel mechanisms can win (Cobenfy) but the schizophrenia graveyard is deep, and regulators/payers now have a non-D2 option to benchmark against. OV4071's differentiation would be a non-dopaminergic, non-muscarinic, non-sedating mechanism — but clinical differentiation must be earned on psychosis scales in patients, where KCC2 has zero precedent.

KCC2 mechanism — competitive whitespace

  • UNVERIFIED Ovid describes its molecules as the "only known direct KCC2 activators," and independent searches for this dossier found no other clinical-stage direct KCC2 activator. If true, OV4071/OV350 face no direct-mechanism competition — but equally, no competitor will have de-risked the target. Indirect approaches (e.g., NKCC1 inhibition with bumetanide, WNK-SPAK pathway modulators) remain preclinical/academic. Company deck; literature search Oct 2026.

CCM (for completeness — program paused)

  • Runway into 2029 holds; ATM used opportunistically rather than defensively.
