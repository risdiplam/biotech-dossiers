The key swing factor: whether the sham-controlled Phase 3 EMPEROR study replicates the open-label 59–91% seizure-reduction signal at the week-28 primary endpoint in Q3 2027. The entire valuation rests on this single readout.

INTERPRETATION Bull case logic. Zorevunersen is a first-in-class disease-modifying candidate for a severe genetic epilepsy: it upregulates the patient's own SCN1A gene rather than suppressing seizures symptomatically. The open-label dataset (81 patients in Phase 1/2a; 75 in extension; some treated >5 years; NEJM publication March 2026) shows durable major-motor seizure reductions plus cognition/behavior gains versus natural history — a profile no approved Dravet therapy offers. Biogen's $165M-upfront partnership (Feb 2025) validates the asset and funds 30% of development costs, and pro-forma cash of $420M funds operations through a potential early-2028 US launch without a forced raise. The completed FDA meeting (Oct 2026) de-risks the regulatory path incrementally: the agency agreed to the multicomponent Vineland-3 analysis plan for the cognition secondary.

INTERPRETATION Bear case logic. This is a single-asset binary with a fully-diluted valuation near $2B already pricing in substantial Phase 3 success. All efficacy data to date are open-label with no placebo control; caregiver-reported cognition/behavior endpoints are vulnerable to expectation bias. The direct competitor Encoded Therapeutics (ETX101, one-time AAV gene therapy, $275M Series F, pivotal data end 2027) could reset the standard of care even if EMPEROR hits. And the precedent of soticlestat — which narrowly missed its Dravet Phase 3 primary (p=0.06) — shows how thin the margin is in this indication.

GAP EMPEROR's formal powering assumptions and any interim-analysis provisions remain undisclosed in accessible sources; the exact statistical bar for "success" at week 28 beyond the log-transformed primary endpoint definition is not verifiable publicly.

Plain-language mechanism

FACT Most human genes waste a fraction of their RNA output: during splicing, cells sometimes include a "poison exon" (also called an NMD exon) that carries a premature stop signal, sending that transcript to nonsense-mediated decay instead of making protein. TANGO uses a short synthetic antisense oligonucleotide that binds the pre-mRNA and blocks inclusion of the poison exon, so more transcripts become productive — raising protein output from the patient's healthy gene copy. Because it only boosts the normal allele, the approach is restricted to haploinsufficiency diseases where ~50% of normal protein is lost. Company disclosures; Han et al., Sci Transl Med 2020 (see references)

FACT In Dravet syndrome, ~85% of cases are caused by de novo heterozygous variants in SCN1A, which encodes the NaV1.1 sodium-channel subunit. NaV1.1 is concentrated in GABAergic inhibitory interneurons — the brain's "brakes." Losing half the channels disinhibits cortical networks, producing frequent prolonged seizures in infancy, developmental stagnation, cognitive/behavioral impairment, ataxia, and elevated risk of sudden unexpected death in epilepsy (SUDEP). Company PR; insideprecisionmedicine summary of the field

FACT Zorevunersen is an intrathecally delivered ASO designed to silence the conserved SCN1A poison exon, increasing SCN1A mRNA and NaV1.1 protein in the brain. STK-002 applies the same TANGO logic to OPA1 in autosomal dominant optic atrophy (intravitreal delivery), upregulating OPA1 from the wild-type allele to improve mitochondrial function in retinal ganglion cells. Company PRs; UVA/Stoke AES 2020 poster

Technical depth — the TANGO pharmacology

  • INTERPRETATION TANGO ASOs are steric-blocking oligonucleotides (non-RNase-H-recruiting by design intent): they hybridize to sequences flanking the poison exon in the pre-mRNA and redirect spliceosome choice toward productive splicing, rather than degrading a target transcript the way gapmer ASOs do. The effect is protein upregulation from the endogenous wild-type allele — no gene transfer, no viral vector, no editing machinery.
  • INTERPRETATION The pharmacology is inherently dose-titratable and reversible (repeat dosing maintains effect; stopping dosing washes it out), contrasting with AAV gene-therapy competitors (ETX101) that are irreversible and non-re-dosable. The trade-off is chronic re-dosing: intrathecal administration every few months for zorevunersen, with post-lumbar-puncture syndrome reported in ~25% of Phase 1/2a patients.
  • FACT The SCN1A poison exon is conserved across human, monkey, mouse and rat (Han et al. 2020) — the conserved target sequence is what made the translatable mouse pharmacology package possible and is the basis for the cross-species dosing rationale. Han et al., Sci Transl Med 2020
  • INTERPRETATION A subtle but important design feature: because TANGO boosts only the healthy allele, it cannot help the ~15% of Dravet patients whose disease is not SCN1A-driven, and patients carrying SCN1A gain-of-function variants are excluded from EMPEROR (per the Oct 2026 study-details disclosure) — a loss-of-function enhancer could theoretically worsen a gain-of-function phenotype.

Academic literature (beyond company materials)

  • Han et al., Science Translational Medicine, August 2020 (Zhou Han, Stoke, with Lori Isom, Univ. of Michigan). FACT Identified a poison exon in SCN1A conserved across human, monkey, mouse and rat; screened 47 ASOs in human neural progenitor cells; lead compound STK-001 selectively raised NaV1.1 without affecting other sodium-channel genes. In Scn1a+/− Dravet mice dosed by intracerebroventricular injection: treatment at postnatal day 2 → 97% (33/34) survival to day 90 vs 23% (14/62) with placebo (p<0.0001); treatment at day 14 → 85% (45/53) vs 64% (47/74) (p<0.005); EEG monitoring days 22–46 → 76% (16/21) seizure-free vs 48% (10/21) with placebo. BusinessWire summary of the publication; The Transmitter coverage
  • Wengert et al. (Univ. of Virginia / Manoj Patel lab; Stoke collaborators), presented AES 2020. FACT Showed STK-001 rescues the reduced intrinsic excitability of parvalbumin-positive (PV) inhibitory interneurons in Scn1a+/− mice to wild-type levels — the first direct evidence that TANGO rescues the core cellular disease mechanism, not just seizures. A single infant-mouse treatment prevented seizures and premature death. Inside Precision Medicine; Stoke AES 2020 poster

Why it could fail — scientific risks

  1. INTERPRETATION Therapeutic window narrows with age. The mouse data show dramatically larger effects with earlier treatment (P2 vs P14). EMPEROR enrolls children and adolescents — years past the early-developmental window where interneuron circuitry is most plastic. Human benefit on cognition may be smaller than the mouse data imply.
  2. INTERPRETATION Delivery to the right cells. Intrathecal ASO must reach deep cortical and hippocampal PV interneurons at sufficient concentration. The 45% rate of CSF protein elevation in the OLE suggests meaningful compartmental pharmacology whose long-term significance is unknown.
  3. INTERPRETATION Open-label inflation. Every human efficacy data point is open-label. Caregiver- and clinician-reported global impression and Vineland-3 scores are susceptible to expectation bias; EMPEROR is the first placebo-controlled test.
  4. INTERPRETATION Genetic heterogeneity. TANGO boosts the healthy allele. Patients carry diverse SCN1A variants (truncations vs missense); whether missense-variant carriers respond equally is unproven in humans. Trial eligibility requires a confirmed mutation; variant-type breakdowns are not public. GAP
  5. INTERPRETATION Lifelong invasive dosing vs one-time alternatives. Zorevunersen requires repeat lumbar-puncture dosing every few months for life. If Encoded's one-time AAV (ETX101) proves durable, the dosing-burden comparison becomes a commercial and adherence headwind — though ASOs are re-dosable and reversible, which AAV is not.
  6. FACT ~15% of Dravet cases are not SCN1A-driven and are mechanistically unaddressable by zorevunersen. Company/field disclosures

EMPEROR — global registrational Phase 3 (NCT06872125)

  • FACT Population: children aged 2 to <18 years with Dravet syndrome and a confirmed SCN1A variant not associated with gain of function, on stable background anti-seizure medications. Company PR, 30 Jun 2026; Practical Neurology, Oct 2026 (from 5 Oct 2026 company release)
  • FACT Randomization: 1:1 zorevunersen (intrathecal) vs sham comparator for a 52-week treatment period following baseline; double-blind. Company PR, 5 Oct 2026
  • FACT Enrollment: 162 patients randomized (target was 150); enrollment completed June 2026; zero discontinuations reported to date. The design had assumed ~15% discontinuation, partly because the control arm undergoes a sham lumbar-puncture procedure. Company remains blinded. Company PR, 30 Jun 2026; CEO remarks at Canaccord, Aug 2026
  • FACT Dosing: two 70 mg intrathecal loading doses by week 8, followed by two 45 mg doses over the 52-week study period. CEO remarks at Canaccord, Aug 2026
  • FACT Primary endpoint (clarified Oct 2026): estimated change from baseline in log-transformed major motor seizure frequency at week 28. Key secondaries: durability of seizure-frequency response at week 52, and change in adaptive functioning (cognition/behavior) at week 52. Company PR, 5 Oct 2026
  • FACT Cognition analysis plan (new, post-FDA meeting): multicomponent assessment of four prespecified Vineland-3 subdomains — expressive communication, receptive communication, interpersonal relationships, personal skills — selected with clinician/caregiver input for their potential to show meaningful effects within the one-year treatment period; analyzed using a multivariate mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) for the US NDA. Company PR, 5 Oct 2026
  • FACT Development economics: Stoke leads with Biogen cost-sharing 70/30. Company PRs; Biogen agreement (10-Q)
  • GAP Formal powering assumptions and any interim-analysis provisions remain undisclosed in accessible sources.
  • INTERPRETATION Zero dropouts in a sham-LP pediatric trial is operationally exceptional and modestly de-risks the tolerability question — but it also raises expectations, making the binary sharper: the market will read a clean, fully-enrolled, well-retained trial as having removed execution excuses.

Safety signals to date

  • FACT Most common TEAEs: post-lumbar-puncture syndrome (~25% in Phase 1/2a); CSF protein increase (45% in OLE) — with no cases of increased intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus. Of serious TEAEs, one was considered treatment-related. One suspected serious adverse reaction led to study withdrawal. FACT Two deaths from SUDEP and one from malnutrition were reported in the study population. NEJM Mar 2026 (via Northwestern/Practical Neurology summaries)
  • INTERPRETATION The SUDEP deaths occurred in a population with high baseline SUDEP risk and the program's thesis is SUDEP-risk reduction via fewer severe seizures; causality cannot be assessed from open-label data. The CSF-protein signal warrants watching in EMPEROR but has not produced clinical sequelae to date.
