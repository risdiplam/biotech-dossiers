03 / SCIENCE

The disease chain—and where a drug can break it

DM1 is not simply low DMPK. Expanded CUG RNA is retained in nuclear foci, sequesters MBNL proteins, and drives a broad fetal-splicing reversion across muscle, heart, and brain.

01

CTG expansion

Expanded repeats in the DMPK 3′ UTR generate toxic CUG-repeat RNA. Repeat size and phenotype correlate only probabilistically because of somatic mosaicism and tissue differences.

02

Nuclear RNA foci

Expanded transcripts are retained in nuclei. Mouse models expressing CUG repeats outside the DMPK locus still develop myotonia and myopathy.

03

MBNL sequestration

MBNL proteins bind the repeats and become functionally depleted; CELF1 dysregulation adds a second splicing disturbance.

04

Program-level spliceopathy

CLCN1, BIN1, SCN5A, INSR and many other transcripts revert toward fetal isoforms, mapping to myotonia, weakness, conduction disease, and systemic features.

Strongest causal anchor

Correcting one splice event can correct one phenotype

In mice, morpholino correction of Clcn1 splicing eliminated myotonia while other mis-splicing and nuclear foci persisted. That validates splicing as causal for a specific symptom—but also shows that one corrected transcript is not the whole disease.

FACT · MOUSE Wheeler et al., JCI 2007.

Clinical bridge

Molecular correction has correlated with function

In MARINA, inferred functional MBNL change correlated with QMT change (r=0.3612, p=0.0499) and vHOT change (r=−0.4250, p=0.0216). These are supportive associations from an early-stage study, not proof of Phase 3 success.

04 / CLINICAL

PGN-EDODM1: a clean mechanism, an unresolved dose curve

PepGen conjugates an arginine-rich cell-penetrating peptide to a neutral PMO. The PMO binds the expanded CUG tract and frees MBNL without cutting DMPK RNA.

Steric block in 60 seconds INTERPRETATION

Plain English. DM1 muscle cells trap the splicing protein MBNL on toxic CUG-repeat RNA. PGN-EDODM1 is a short synthetic oligo (a molecular “patch”) attached to a cell-penetrating peptide that drags it into muscle nuclei. It sticks to the repeat RNA and covers it, freeing MBNL to resume its normal splicing job—without destroying the DMPK transcript itself.

Technical. A neutral phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomer (PMO), complementary to the expanded CUG tract, conjugated to PepGen’s EDO (enhanced delivery oligonucleotide) arginine-rich peptide for systemic muscle delivery. Binding is steric blockade of MBNL sequestration, not RNase-mediated degradation: mutant and wild-type DMPK RNA are preserved, avoiding the haploinsufficiency concern attached to degrader competitors.

1 · Delivery at a tolerable dose

The peptide must get enough PMO into human muscle nuclei at 10–12.5 mg/kg. Mouse proof (Pip6a-PMO) does not establish human therapeutic index.

2 · Renal therapeutic index

Arginine-rich PPMOs carry a renal/electrolyte class signal; a mild transient kidney-biomarker DLT already appeared at 15 mg/kg SAD. Repeat dosing can narrow the window.

3 · Splicing → function break

MARINA’s MBNL–function correlations were modest (r ≈ 0.36–0.43); CELF1 dysregulation is untouched by steric blockade. Correction can be real and still not move function.

4 · Cardiac delivery gap

Cardiac muscle is consistently harder for PPMOs to reach than skeletal muscle, while conduction disease is a major cause of DM1 mortality. Cardiac target engagement at clinical dose is not publicly established.

Safety signal to carry forward

At 15 mg/kg SAD, one transient kidney-biomarker elevation met the protocol definition of a dose-limiting toxicity but was classified as mild and resolved without intervention. Earlier 10 mg/kg data included one treatment-related SAE of abdominal pain, described as potentially confounded by prohibited off-label medication.

What FREEDOM2 has shown so far

UNVERIFIED As of August 6, PepGen reported no SAEs or dose-limiting toxicities in FREEDOM2 or the OLE, no cumulative toxicity at 5 or 10 mg/kg, and no treatment discontinuations.

What remains unknowable

GAP Public data do not establish durability, dose-response under repeat dosing, patient-level consistency, or whether higher molecular correction will convert to functional separation.

INTERPRETATION

The platform diligence question is therapeutic index

Peptide conjugation can transform systemic PMO uptake in DM1 mouse models, but arginine-rich PPMOs carry a renal/electrolyte class signal. PepGen has no identified peer-reviewed EDO platform paper, so product-specific NHP renal data at multiples of the intended dose remain the central preclinical evidence gap.

GAP

Cardiac delivery

Cardiac muscle is consistently harder for PPMOs to reach than skeletal muscle, while cardiac conduction disease is a major cause of DM1 mortality. Clinical-dose cardiac target engagement for PGN-EDODM1 is not publicly established in peer-reviewed evidence.

FACT · MOUSE

Proof that peptide delivery can matter

In HSALR mice, Pip6a-PMO-CAG7 corrected splicing after systemic dosing; three doses abolished myotonia and corrected 85% of dysregulated transcripts. Mouse efficacy does not establish human therapeutic index.

06 / HARBOR

What failed—and what the press release cannot tell us

HARBOR was a 159-person, 54-week Phase 3 trial of q8w del-desiran. The primary vHOT endpoint missed. Everything else central to mechanism interpretation remains undisclosed.

Known

  • Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 159 participants.
  • Seven IV doses, every eight weeks; primary endpoint vHOT through Week 54.
  • No statistically significant improvement versus placebo on vHOT.
  • Novartis said secondary and exploratory activity was observed, without numbers.
  • Safety was described as generally consistent with prior data.

Unknown

  • vHOT effect size, confidence interval, or p-value.
  • HARBOR dose.
  • DMPK knockdown and splicing correction at 54 weeks.
  • Magnitude and consistency of secondary-endpoint effects.
  • Whether Novartis will continue, redesign, or stop the program.
September 30 update: Dyne’s one-year ACHIEVE data. UNVERIFIED · COMPANY Dyne reported that a pooled ACHIEVE MAD group (N=25–26; 3.4 mg/kg Q4W plus 5.4 and 6.8 mg/kg Q8W) showed vHOT improvement versus placebo at 6 months (−3.2 s vs +0.4 s, placebo N=14) and, at 12 months, functional improvement versus baseline and versus a propensity-matched END-DM1 natural-history cohort across multiple measures (MDHI only versus an unmatched cohort). There is no 12-month placebo comparison: placebo participants were re-baselined onto active drug, and only 8 of 26 received the 6.8 mg/kg registrational dose for the full 12 months (Dyne press release, Sept. 29, 2026). INTERPRETATION This partially rehabilitates vHOT as a measurable clinical endpoint—it is no longer “dead” post-HARBOR—but it does not answer the degrader question: z-basivarsen degrades DMPK, exactly like the failed del-desiran. The steric-block versus degrader distinction for PepGen remains untested.

The evidence that would change the view

A deep dive should end with falsifiers, not a verdict. These are the highest-value missing pieces as of October 10, 2026.

01

Full HARBOR dataset

Numerical vHOT and secondary results, DMPK knockdown, splicing correction, and patient-level consistency.

02

FREEDOM2 10 mg/kg

Pre-specified analysis, all-patient distribution, assay details, safety, durability, and functional outcomes—not just group means.

03

Hold resolution package

Specific FDA questions, whether new studies are required, and a credible timeline to reopen U.S. sites.

04

Dose-curve integrity

Whether repeat-dose tissue concentration and splicing scale consistently from 5 to 10 and 12.5 mg/kg.

05

Cash and issuance

Q3 2026 cash, remaining ATM capacity, and financing strategy after the November readout.

06

12.5 mg/kg start

Confirmation that the highest-dose cohort enrolled after the August “shortly” guidance.

INTERPRETATION

Current research posture

The sub-$3 setup offers large percentage sensitivity to a successful higher-dose readout because the operating EV is small. But the thesis is not “cash-backed safety”; it is that the market may be overgeneralizing a degrader/vHOT failure to a differentiated steric blocker before the decisive 10 mg/kg data. That hypothesis is coherent, not proven.
