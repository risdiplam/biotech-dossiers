02 / SCIENCE

Blocking the leak at the B2 receptor

Plain-language mechanism, the academic literature behind it, and why it could still fail.

Plain-language mechanism

In HAE types 1 and 2, mutations leave patients with too little (or dysfunctional) C1 esterase inhibitor (C1-INH), the protein that normally puts the brakes on the kallikrein–kinin cascade. Without that brake, plasma kallikrein runs unchecked and cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen, flooding the blood with bradykinin — a short-lived peptide that binds the bradykinin B2 receptor on blood-vessel walls, forcing vessels to leak fluid into tissue. That leak is the swelling attack (skin, gut, airway). A B2 receptor antagonist sits in the receptor's binding pocket and physically blocks bradykinin from docking — shutting off the leak signal at its final step. FACT CHDR / Br J Clin Pharmacol 2010 World Allergy Organ J 2010

Deucrictibant applies this same receptor-level blockade with a small molecule instead of a peptide, which is what makes it orally bioavailable — and what allows two formulations: an immediate-release capsule for fast attack treatment and an extended-release tablet for round-the-clock prophylactic coverage. FACT Q2 2026 PR

Technical depth — what the molecule actually does

Deucrictibant is a competitive B2 antagonist: it occupies the receptor's orthosteric binding pocket without activating it, so blockade is surmountable in principle — a large enough bradykinin surge can displace it (the basis of failure mode #5 below). Icatibant, the peptide predecessor, is likewise competitive, but as a synthetic decapeptide it cannot survive the gut — hence subcutaneous injection, a ~1–2 h half-life, and an on-demand-only existence. The small-molecule chemistry is what makes both rapid IR absorption (attack treatment) and sustained XR exposure (once-daily prophylaxis) possible from one active ingredient — and it is also what introduces the off-target toxicology risk that a peptide never carried. FACT CHDR 2010 INTERPRETATION

Published potency metrics (Ki / IC50 vs the human B2 receptor) for deucrictibant were not among the sources cited in this dossier — treat any such figures encountered elsewhere as company-reported, not independently verified here. GAP

Why the target is considered validated

  • Icatibant (Firazyr, now generic) — a synthetic decapeptide B2 antagonist — proved the mechanism in humans: selective, competitive B2 blockade, approved for acute HAE attacks (subcutaneous injection; FAST-1/FAST-2 Phase 3 trials). Icatibant is the clinical proof that blocking B2 stops an attack in progress. FACT CHDR 2010 World Allergy Organ J 2010
  • A 2026 review in Clinical Reviews in Allergy & Immunology summarizing the evidence for the bradykinin B2 receptor as a therapeutic target in bradykinin-mediated angioedema (HAE and AAE-C1INH) was cited by Pharvaris in its Q2 2026 business update. FACT Q2 2026 PR
  • Complementary validation comes from upstream: plasma kallikrein inhibitors (ecallantide, lanadelumab, berotralstat, sebetralstat) and C1-INH replacement all work by reducing the same bradykinin signal, confirming the cascade is the causal pathway. INTERPRETATION

Why it could fail — ranked

  1. Chronic B2 blockade is clinically unproven. Icatibant was only ever tested as an acute, on-demand therapy (it is injected, with a 1–2 h half-life); no B2 antagonist has been used for long-term prophylaxis in humans before deucrictibant XR. Whether continuous receptor antagonism stays effective and tolerable over years — without tachyphylaxis, receptor upregulation, or off-target small-molecule toxicity — is the central scientific unknown. INTERPRETATION
  2. Prophylaxis demands 24/7 coverage. An on-demand drug only needs to beat an attack to the receptor; a once-daily prophylactic must maintain sufficient receptor occupancy around the clock. PK dips, missed doses, or GI variability could leave windows of vulnerability that a trial's controlled setting masks. INTERPRETATION
  3. Small-molecule off-target risk already bit once. The FDA put deucrictibant studies on clinical hold in Aug 2022 (reason undisclosed; resolved with a 26-week rodent toxicology study, holds lifted Jun 2023 for on-demand and Jan 2024 for prophylaxis). A chronic-use small molecule carries a different tox profile than an injected peptide, and regulators will scrutinize long-term exposure data. FACT BioSpace / Rare Disease Advisor
  4. HAE with normal C1INH is mechanistically heterogeneous. CHAPTER-3 enrolled HAE types 1, 2, and normal-C1INH patients, but normal-C1INH HAE has multiple genetic subtypes (FXII, plasminogen, kininogen, etc.) whose attacks may not all be purely bradykinin-driven. A B2 antagonist could underperform in that subgroup and narrow the label. INTERPRETATION
  5. Downstream blockade leaves upstream activity intact. Unlike kallikrein or FXIIa inhibitors, B2 antagonism does not reduce bradykinin production itself; massive bradykinin surges could theoretically outcompete a competitive antagonist during severe attacks (e.g., laryngeal). INTERPRETATION

What the science does NOT yet prove: full CHAPTER-3 and RAPIDe-3 datasets are topline only — subgroup breakouts (normal-C1INH patients, adolescents, laryngeal attacks), exposure–response, and long-term safety data have not been published as of Oct 7, 2026. GAP
