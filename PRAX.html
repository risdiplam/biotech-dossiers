Thesis in one paragraph

Praxis is attempting a rare pre-revenue double launch: two NDAs under FDA review (relutrigine for ultra-rare SCN2A/SCN8A developmental epileptic encephalopathies; ulixacaltamide for essential tremor, a ~7M-patient US population with no purpose-built drug approved in decades), a Phase 3 broad-DEE readout (EMERALD, Q4 2026) that could support a 2027 sNDA, and an ASO registrational program (elsunersen, EMBRAVE3, 2027) — all funded by ~$1.4B of cash raised across four public offerings since January 2024. INTERPRETATION The investment case is a concentrated clinical-regulatory binary, not a commercial story: valuation (~$7.2B basic / ~$8.2B fully diluted) already prices in substantial approval probability, and the next ~80 days (two PDUFAs five weeks apart) resolve most of the variance. 2 · Science: Mechanism of Action & Why It Could Fail

2a · Ulixacaltamide (PRAX-944) — selective T-type calcium-channel inhibitor, essential tremor

Plain-language MoA. Essential tremor is thought to arise from pathological rhythmic "burst" firing of neurons in the cerebello-thalamo-cortical (CTC) circuit. T-type calcium channels (CaV3.1/3.2/3.3) are low-voltage-activated channels that drive the rebound burst firing underlying these oscillations; they are densely expressed at every CTC node — deep cerebellar nuclei, Purkinje cells, ventral thalamic nuclei (including VIM, where burst firing recorded in ET patients correlates with tremor), and motor cortex. Ulixacaltamide is a highly selective small-molecule T-type calcium-channel blocker designed to suppress neuronal bursting in this circuit. FACT (company disclosures; review literature)

Academic foundation.

  • Park et al., PNAS 2010 (PMID 20498062): demonstrated that potentiation of CaV3.1 T-type channels in the inferior olive drives tremor onset in the harmaline pharmacologic model of essential tremor; CaV3.1 knockout mice lacked rhythmic burst discharges, and selective shRNA knockdown of CaV3.1 in the inferior olive suppressed harmaline-induced tremor. FACT
  • Handforth et al., Neuropharmacology 2010 (PMID 20547167 / PMC3787716): five T-type calcium-channel antagonists (ethosuximide, zonisamide, ECN, KYS05064, NNC 55-0396) suppressed tremor ≥50% in the harmaline model and ≥70% in the GABAA α1-null model — but CaV3.1-null mice exhibited as much tremor as wild-type, and two antagonists suppressed tremor equally in null mice, leading the authors to conclude "it is uncertain whether medications developed to block only the CaV3.1 subtype would exhibit efficacy." FACT — this is a genuine scientific caveat for a subtype-selective blocker.
  • Review literature (e.g., "T-type calcium channels as therapeutic targets in essential tremor and Parkinson's disease"): CACNA1G (CaV3.1) variants reported in three ET families showed little-to-no functional change vs wild type; the traditional olivary model of ET is "increasingly in question," with the broader CTC network now centered in ET pathophysiology. FACT

Why it could fail.

  • Mechanism graveyard in the same indication: Jazz Pharmaceuticals' suvecaltamide — a highly selective, state-dependent T-type calcium-channel modulator — missed its primary endpoint (TETRAS modified composite at week 12) and key secondary (CGI-S) in a 420-patient Phase 2b (NCT05122650), reported June 2024, with placebo overperforming expectations. Sage/Biogen's SAGE-324 (GABAA PAM, different mechanism) also failed Phase 2 (KINETIC 2, n=147) in ET and was terminated July 2024. The short-seller report notes three same-mechanism early ET failures. FACT INTERPRETATION Same-target precedent is negative; ulixacaltamide's differentiation rests on trial design (functional mADL endpoint) and molecule, not on a validated target.
  • Prior miss by this drug: the Phase 2b Essential1 trial (n=132) missed its primary mADL endpoint (LS mean difference −1.58; 95% CI −3.60 to 0.45; p=0.126, March 2023), with only nominal significance on TETRAS-ADL (p=0.026). The Phase 3 success came after endpoint/timing optimization (primary at week 8 rather than week 12). FACT
  • Durability unknown: Study 2's randomized-withdrawal maintenance signal spans only 4 weeks in pre-selected responders; Study 1's primary is at week 8 of a 12-week trial. Long-term functional durability in a chronic, progressive, elderly disease is unproven. INTERPRETATION
  • Tolerability in the target population: ~27–28% AE discontinuations and TEAEs including dizziness, "brain fog," cognitive complaints, and euphoric mood in an older, comorbid population — a plausible labeling and adherence headwind. FACT (NeurologyLive) INTERPRETATION

2b · Relutrigine (PRAX-562) — preferential persistent-sodium-current inhibitor, SCN2A/SCN8A DEEs

Plain-language MoA. SCN2A and SCN8A encode the neuronal voltage-gated sodium channels NaV1.2 and NaV1.6. Gain-of-function variants increase a small but physiologically critical "persistent" sodium current (INaP) — channels that fail to fully inactivate and leak depolarizing current — producing neuronal hyperexcitability and severe early-onset epilepsy (developmental and epileptic encephalopathies). Relutrigine is a first-in-class small molecule that preferentially inhibits this persistent current with selectivity for disease-state (hyperexcitable) channels, rather than blocking the peak transient current that drives normal action potentials. FACT (company; AES abstract)

Academic foundation.

  • Veeramah/Meisler et al. (SCN8A discovery, Am. J. Hum. Genet. 2012): de novo heterozygous SCN8A missense (p.Asn1768Asp) caused a dramatic increase in persistent sodium current with incomplete inactivation and a dominant gain-of-function phenotype; knock-in mice recapitulated seizures and SUDEP — foundational evidence linking GoF persistent-current elevation to DEE. FACT
  • Children 2025 review (MDPI, "Precision Therapeutics in Lennox–Gastaut Syndrome"): standard sodium-channel blockers (carbamazepine, phenytoin) inhibit fast inactivation/transient currents but "do not significantly affect persistent sodium currents, which prolong neuronal depolarization and contribute to hyperexcitability"; blocking transient currents causes dose-dependent ataxia, sedation, and cognitive impairment. This is the mechanistic rationale for a persistent-current-preferential agent. FACT
  • AES abstract (Scott, Hughes, Kahlig et al., Praxis): PRAX-562 inhibits persistent INa with improved selectivity over peak INa vs carbamazepine and lamotrigine, with dose-dependent seizure inhibition up to complete control in SCN2A/SCN8A and other DEE mouse models. FACT (conference abstract, company-affiliated authors)

Why it could fail.

  • Thin registrational dataset: the NDA rests substantially on a single n=51 cohort stopped early for efficacy — early stopping systematically inflates effect estimates, and the FDA's major-amendment extension (sensitivity analyses) signals the agency is probing the data's robustness. INTERPRETATION
  • Genetic heterogeneity: EMERALD broadens to >50 genetic etiologies; the precision rationale (GoF persistent-current) is strongest in SCN2A/SCN8A and dilutes across all-cause DEE. A positive EMBOLD does not de-risk EMERALD. INTERPRETATION
  • Small-population commercial and diagnostic friction — kept lean per the clinical-binary focus: ultra-rare, pediatric, diagnosis-dependent; relevant only post-approval. INTERPRETATION

2c · Vormatrigine (PRAX-628) — functional-state sodium-channel modulator, focal onset seizures

Vormatrigine is a sodium-channel modulator designed for preferential activity at hyperexcitable (disease-state) channels — conceptually the adult-focal-epilepsy sibling of relutrigine's approach. FACT In the open-label RADIANT study it showed rapid, durable signals (56.3% median seizure-frequency reduction over 8 weeks; ~22% seizure-free in the final 28 days), but the randomized Phase 2/3 POWER1 trial missed its primary endpoint (percent change in monthly seizure frequency) in highly refractory patients, and POWER2 enrollment is paused pending program redesign. FACT INTERPRETATION The randomized failure materially weakens the "functional-state selectivity differentiates in FOS" thesis; in a crowded generic anti-seizure-medication market, the bar for differentiation is high and now unmet on the primary endpoint.

2d · Elsunersen (PRAX-222) — SCN2A-lowering antisense oligonucleotide

An intrathecally delivered ASO (Solidus™ platform, Ionis collaboration) that lowers SCN2A expression to counter gain-of-function SCN2A-DEE — a directly disease-targeted, potentially disease-modifying approach. EMBRAVE Part A showed a 77% sham-adjusted reduction in monthly seizures (p=0.015) sustained in open-label extension up to one year; FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy Designation (June 22, 2026); EMBRAVE3 is a streamlined single-arm registrational study (~30 patients, 24-week countable-motor-seizure endpoint) with topline expected 2027. FACT INTERPRETATION Why it could fail: intrathecal delivery burden in infants/children, tiny single-arm registrational dataset, and ASO platform risk (no established clinical efficacy for Praxis's earlier ASO programs PRAX-080/-090/-100). The relutrigine small molecule, if approved, would also compete for the same SCN2A GoF patients.

4 · Trial Design Deep Dive

Essential3 — ulixacaltamide in essential tremor (NCT06087276), topline Oct 16, 2025

Design. Two concurrently enrolled, decentralized US studies in adults with ET on ≤1 stable ET medication, randomized 2:1 to Study 1 or Study 2. Study 1: 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group trial (n=473 enrolled; primary analysis populations reported as n=199 ulixacaltamide / n=233 placebo in one summary), 1:1 randomization. Primary endpoint: change from baseline to week 8 in Modified Activities of Daily Living 11 (mADL11; 0–33 scale, higher = greater tremor impact on daily function). Study 2: randomized-withdrawal design — 238 patients received 8 weeks open-label ulixacaltamide; the 80 responders (≥3-point mADL11 improvement) were re-randomized 1:1 (40/40) to continue drug or switch to placebo for 4 weeks; primary endpoint: maintenance of response. FACT

Safety. Generally well tolerated per company; no drug-related serious AEs. Most common TEAEs (≥10%): constipation, dizziness, euphoric mood, brain fog, headache, paraesthesia, insomnia. EMBOLD — relutrigine in SCN2A/SCN8A DEEs, registrational cohort (AES, Dec 6, 2025)

Design. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled registrational cohort in pediatric SCN2A/SCN8A DEE patients; 16-week double-blind period. Primary endpoint: percent/absolute reduction in motor seizure frequency (log-transformed change from baseline, ANCOVA — the analysis method agreed with FDA). FACT

Results (n=51, company-reported). 53% placebo-adjusted reduction in motor seizures over 16 weeks (p<0.0002); 66% increase in motor seizure-free days (p=0.034); broad functional improvements across behavior, alertness, communication, overall status (p≤0.002, caregiver/clinician measures); rapid, significant, and increasing seizure reduction over time; no drug-related serious AEs; treatment-related AEs predominantly mild–moderate. The independent Data Monitoring Committee recommended stopping early for efficacy. FACT

Prior cohort (Sep 2024, n=22: 15 drug / 7 placebo). 46% placebo-adjusted monthly motor seizure reduction (LS mean log difference −0.585, SE 0.259; ANCOVA p=0.0354); >30% of relutrigine patients achieved seizure freedom in a 28-day window; open-label extension median 75–77% seizure-rate reduction with 5 patients seizure-free. FACT

Statistical caveats. The registrational inference rests on a single small cohort with an early efficacy stop — the classic setup for effect-size inflation — and the FDA's request for additional sensitivity analyses (the major amendment extending PDUFA) indicates the agency is stress-testing exactly this fragility. INTERPRETATION Powering assumptions and interim alpha-spending boundaries were not disclosed publicly. GAP

EMERALD — relutrigine in broad DEEs (NCT07010471), Phase 3

Design (AAN 2025 abstract; company updates). Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; ages 2–65 with DEE diagnosis and seizure onset <12 years; ≥4 motor seizures in 4-week baseline; 1:1 randomization to relutrigine (1 mg/kg/day starting, up to 1.5 mg/kg/day QD) or placebo; Part A 16-week double-blind, Part B 32-week open-label extension, plus safety follow-up; home/in-clinic assessment options. Target ~160; enrollment exceeded target at ~200 patients across >50 genetic etiologies. Topline Q4 2026 (company guidance); if positive, supports sNDA in 2027. FACT

POWER1 — vormatrigine in focal onset seizures (NCT06999902), topline Jun 1, 2026

Design. Phase 2/3, double-blind, randomized, multicenter; adults with FOS on 1–3 background anti-seizure medications; randomized to vormatrigine 20 mg QD for 6 weeks then 30 mg QD for 6 weeks, or placebo for 12 weeks. Primary endpoint: percent change in monthly seizure frequency from baseline — not met. Key secondary 50% responder rate met; seizure reduction more pronounced in the second half at 30 mg. Generally well tolerated; AE discontinuations <10%; ~90% of the active arm entered the open-label extension. Enrollment in POWER2 paused for program reassessment. FACT (company 8-K/PR)

RADIANT (open-label, vormatrigine, FOS + generalized epilepsy; AES Dec 2025). 56.3% median seizure-frequency reduction over 8 weeks; ~22% seizure-free in the final 28 days; company reported median reduction "reaching 100% for patients continuing through 16 weeks" — a conditional, completer-only framing. FACT INTERPRETATION Open-label completer analyses cannot be compared to randomized results; the POWER1 primary miss is the dispositive randomized evidence.

EMBRAVE / EMBRAVE3 — elsunersen in SCN2A GoF DEE

EMBRAVE Part A: 77% sham-adjusted reduction in monthly seizures (p=0.015), benefit sustained in open-label extension up to one year, with broad improvements in severely affected children (company-reported, June 2026). EMBRAVE3: pivotal single-arm registrational study (~30 patients), 24-week countable-motor-seizure endpoint, pathway aligned with FDA; enrolling; topline expected 2027. FACT

6 · Competition: Clinical Differentiation Only

Essential tremor

Approved / established. Propranolol — the only drug FDA-approved for essential tremor (1967, originally a coronary-disease beta blocker); effective in only ~50% of patients with high discontinuation from limited efficacy and poor tolerability FACT (Nature industry feature). Primidone (off-label standard), topiramate, gabapentin — all generic, all repurposed. Device options: deep brain stimulation (VIM) and MR-guided focused ultrasound — invasive or lesional, reserved for severe/refractory patients. FACT

Failed investigational (clinical differentiation context).

  • Suvecaltamide (Jazz/Cavion) — highly selective, state-dependent T-type calcium-channel modulator; Phase 2b (n=420) missed primary (TETRAS modified composite, week 12) and key secondary (CGI-S) at 30 mg vs placebo; placebo overperformed; well tolerated. Program future undecided; Jazz acquired Cavion for $52.5M upfront (2019). FACT — the closest mechanistic comp, and it failed on a tremor-severity scale rather than a functional ADL scale.
  • SAGE-324/BIIB124 (Sage/Biogen) — GABAA positive allosteric modulator; Phase 2 KINETIC 2 (n=147) showed no significant dose-response on TETRAS-PS Item 4 at day 91; ET development terminated July 2024. Different mechanism; relevant as evidence of ET's high trial-failure rate. FACT
  • CX-8998 (Cavion) — earlier T-type blocker; failed in early ET work (cited in short report; detailed results not re-verified here). UNVERIFIED

Differentiation summary. If approved, ulixacaltamide would be the first purpose-built ET pharmacotherapy in decades and the only agent with registrational data on a functional (ADL-based) primary endpoint rather than a tremor-severity scale — a clinically meaningful distinction for a disease defined by functional disability. INTERPRETATION No cross-trial efficacy comparisons are made here: the failed programs used different endpoints (TETRAS), populations, and durations.

SCN2A/SCN8A developmental epileptic encephalopathies

No approved targeted therapy exists for SCN2A- or SCN8A-DEE. FACT Standard care is empiric polypharmacy with broad anti-seizure medications. The mechanistically adjacent class — conventional sodium-channel blockers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, lamotrigine) — inhibits transient/peak sodium current and carries dose-dependent ataxia, sedation, and cognitive impairment; per the reviewed literature they do not meaningfully inhibit the persistent current that GoF variants elevate. FACT (MDPI review) Relutrigine's clinical differentiation, if the data hold, is persistent-current preference with selectivity for hyperexcitable channels — a mechanism no approved or late-stage competitor currently occupies in DEE. INTERPRETATION Elsunersen (Praxis's own ASO) is the only targeted rival in SCN2A GoF, but it is intrathecal, earlier-stage, and internal to the same company.

Focal onset seizures (vormatrigine)

A large, crowded, predominantly generic market (levetiracetam, lacosamide, brivaracetam, cenobamate, perampanel, and others). Newer branded entrants compete on incremental efficacy/tolerability. Following the POWER1 primary miss and POWER2 pause, vormatrigine has no demonstrated randomized differentiation; any future path requires a redesigned trial showing a primary-endpoint win. INTERPRETATION Kept lean: this program is currently option value, not a valuation pillar.

10 · Ranked Red Flags

1. Culper Research short report — unresolved integrity allegations on the lead asset (Nov 2025)

Culper alleged Praxis "engineered" the Essential3 results via a last-minute, unapproved primary-endpoint change (12 weeks → 8 weeks), optimistic imputation for ~36% treatment-arm dropouts (35.6% vs 5.6% placebo all-cause dropout per Culper), and a "half-baked" tipping-point analysis; claimed an independent DMC recommended stopping Study 1 for futility in early 2025 and that three same-mechanism drugs failed in ET. Praxis rebutted via a Nov 24, 2025 fireside chat with external experts; shareholder law firms announced investigations. FACT INTERPRETATION NDA acceptance (Apr 2026) is a partial counter-signal, but acceptance ≠ approval, and the PDUFA review is where these specific claims get tested. This is the #1 swing factor for the stock.

2. Essential1 (Phase 2b) missed its primary endpoint

The same drug on a similar functional endpoint: mADL LS-mean difference −1.58 (95% CI −3.60 to 0.45; p=0.126), March 2023. Phase 3 succeeded after endpoint/timing refinement — a legitimate development path, but it means the registrational win is the second attempt on a re-optimized endpoint, which is exactly the pattern short-sellers flag. FACT

3. Same-target precedent failed: suvecaltamide Phase 2b (Jazz, Jun 2024)

A highly selective, state-dependent T-type calcium-channel modulator missed primary and key secondary in 420 ET patients, with placebo overperformance. Academic literature adds a mechanistic caveat (CaV3.1-null mice tremor equivalence; uncertainty over subtype-selective blockade). Ulixacaltamide's edge is trial design (functional endpoint) and molecule — not a de-risked target. FACT

4. POWER1 primary miss + POWER2 pause (vormatrigine, Jun 2026)

The broad-epilepsy pillar's registrational trial failed its primary endpoint in highly refractory FOS; the program is in redesign with no disclosed restart timeline. The stock's epilepsy diversification narrative now rests almost entirely on relutrigine. FACT

5. Tolerability signal in an elderly population

~27–28% AE discontinuations; TEAEs include dizziness, "brain fog"/cognitive complaints, and euphoric mood — a CNS-active profile in older, comorbid ET patients that could constrain labeling, adherence, and payer positioning even if approved. FACT INTERPRETATION

6. Valuation prices in success with zero revenue

~$7.2B basic / ~$8.2B FD mcap (~$5.8B / ~$6.8B EV) against $0 revenue and ~$80M+ quarterly burn. The Dec 2026 + Jan 2027 PDUFAs are five weeks apart — concentrated, correlated regulatory risk. A CRL on either would force a severe re-rating; cash into 2028 limits near-term financing risk but not valuation risk. INTERPRETATION

7. Thin registrational datasets for relutrigine

NDA anchored on n=51 with an early efficacy stop (effect-inflation risk); the FDA's major-amendment extension confirms the agency is probing robustness. EMERALD's broad-DEE expansion (>50 genotypes) dilutes the precision rationale. INTERPRETATION

8. Insider selling with zero buying; elevated short interest

$0 insider buying vs ~$11.1M selling in the last 12 months (incl. GC sales Nov 2025 and a director's 10b5-1 sales Jun 2026); insider ownership 3.0%; short float 14.45%; beta ~2.8–2.9. FACT Sales appear routine/plan-based, but the complete absence of buying ahead of two PDUFAs is notable. INTERPRETATION

9. Litigation/regulatory overhang

Shareholder law-firm investigations announced post-Culper; any FDA complete response letter would likely revive them. FACT

10. Commercial-execution risk (kept lean)

Pre-revenue company building two launches simultaneously; G&A doubled YoY on commercial build. Relevant only in approval scenarios. INTERPRETATION
