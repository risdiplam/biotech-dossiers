Thesis in one paragraph

SLN is a two-asset siRNA company whose value rests on divesiran, a first-in-class TMPRSS6-silencing siRNA that posted a strong placebo-controlled Phase 2 readout in phlebotomy-dependent polycythemia vera (88% vs 19% responder rate, p<0.0001) with infrequent subcutaneous dosing (Q6W/Q12W) — a genuine binary event with a Phase 3 start guided for 1H 2027. The second leg, zerlasiran, is scientifically Phase 3-ready but commercially stranded: no partner, no self-funded Phase 3, and the entire Lp(a)-lowering class was repriced by pelacarsen's Phase 3 failure (Sep 4, 2026). Cash (~$72M) funds the current burn into roughly 2027 but not a PV Phase 3, so a financing or partnership is the near-term question, not just a clinical one.

The swing factor: whether divesiran's Phase 2 PV effect (small n=48, 36 weeks, surrogate of phlebotomy avoidance/HCT control) reproduces in a larger Phase 3 — and whether that Phase 3 gets funded without heavy dilution.

02 / SCIENCE

Silencing the disease message: how GalNAc-siRNA works — and where it breaks

The platform, the two lead biologies, and the failure modes — with academic literature, not just company slides.

2.1 The platform: GalNAc-conjugated siRNA (mRNAi GOLD™)

Silence's drugs are short interfering RNAs: synthetic double-stranded RNA molecules designed so that one strand (the "guide") loads into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) inside a cell and directs it to cut up the matching messenger RNA — destroying the message before the disease protein is made. The drug does not change the genome; the effect is durable but reversible. INTERPRETATION (standard RNAi mechanism)

Delivery is the whole game for siRNA. Silence conjugates each siRNA to GalNAc (N-acetylgalactosamine), a sugar that binds the asialoglycoprotein receptor (ASGPR), which is highly expressed on liver hepatocytes and negligible elsewhere. The conjugate is taken up by receptor-mediated endocytosis, and chemical stabilization of the siRNA (2′-modifications, phosphorothioate linkages) keeps it active for months after a single subcutaneous dose — hence the quarterly or twice-yearly dosing schedules. FACT Nature Signal Transduction review 2026 Exp Mol Med review

Academic anchors for the delivery science:

  • GalNAc–ASGPR liver targeting: triantennary GalNAc conjugation improved hepatic delivery of oligonucleotides 7- to 10-fold in early ASO work; seven currently approved siRNA drugs use GalNAc conjugates for liver-specific treatment, mostly for hepatic metabolic targets (patisiran excepted, which uses an IV lipid nanoparticle). FACT Nature Sig Transd review
  • A 2026 npj Drug Discovery review summarizes the central translational lesson: patisiran (IV LNP) established liver siRNA in hATTR; subsequent GalNAc conjugates (givosiran, inclisiran) showed receptor-mediated subcutaneous dosing gives potent, durable hepatic silencing — but matching delivery modality to target-organ biology is what separates failures from approvals. Silence's targets are all hepatocyte-expressed (LPA, TMPRSS6, ANGPTL3, GPR146, INHBE), i.e., inside the delivery comfort zone. FACT npj Drug Discovery 2026

2.2 Divesiran biology: TMPRSS6 → hepcidin → iron restriction

Polycythemia vera (PV) is a myeloproliferative neoplasm (usually JAK2-mutated) in which the bone marrow overproduces red blood cells, driving hematocrit (HCT) up. Guidelines target HCT <45% because higher levels raise thrombosis risk; the standard workhorse to control it is therapeutic phlebotomy — literally drawing blood — which is burdensome and induces iron deficiency. FACT (established PV care)

Divesiran silences TMPRSS6, the gene encoding matriptase-2, a liver protease that normally suppresses hepcidin. Removing the brake raises hepcidin, the master iron hormone; hepcidin degrades ferroportin, trapping iron in macrophages/hepatocytes and starving the bone marrow of the iron it needs to make excess red cells. Net effect: fewer red cells, lower HCT, fewer phlebotomies — while iron stores (ferritin) actually improve because iron is redistributed rather than removed. INTERPRETATION of company-reported mechanism, consistent with: Silvestri et al., Cell Metab 2008 (PMID 18976966) — matriptase-2 inhibits hepcidin activation by cleaving membrane hemojuvelin; mutational inactivation causes iron-deficient anemia in mice and humans.

Biology validation in the literature: germline TMPRSS6 loss-of-function causes iron-refractory iron deficiency anemia (IRIDA) in humans — the mirror image of what divesiran is doing pharmacologically, which is strong human-genetic support that TMPRSS6 suppression restricts erythropoiesis. INTERPRETATION (IRIDA genetics are established; the PV therapeutic inference is the company's)

2.3 Zerlasiran biology: LPA silencing → Lp(a) lowering

Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] is an LDL-like particle with an extra apolipoprotein(a) attached, encoded by the LPA gene. Levels are ~90% genetically determined, set by early childhood, and largely unmoved by diet, exercise, or statins. Elevated Lp(a) (≥125 nmol/L per Silence's trial definition) is carried by ~20% of the global population and is epidemiologically and Mendelian-randomization-linked to ASCVD, aortic stenosis, and thrombosis. There is no approved medicine that selectively lowers Lp(a). FACT Q2 2026 10-Q (company disease framing; epidemiology widely published)

Zerlasiran is a GalNAc-siRNA that silences LPA mRNA in hepatocytes, cutting hepatic apo(a) production and hence circulating Lp(a). Phase 2 ALPACAR-360 (n=178, baseline Lp(a) ≥125 nmol/L, median ~215 nmol/L, high ASCVD risk): 300 mg q16w/q24w and 450 mg q24w produced median maximum Lp(a) reductions ≥90% from baseline, highly significant vs placebo to 48 weeks; reductions persisted to the week-60 final visit; well tolerated with no serious safety concerns. Published in JAMA (AHA 2024 presentation). FACT JAMA 2024 / ALPACAR-360 48-week PR

2.4 Why it could fail — the honest biology risks

R-AThe Lp(a) hypothesis is now empirically in doubt. On Sep 4, 2026, Novartis's pelacarsen (antisense, ~72–80% Lp(a) lowering) failed its Phase 3 Lp(a)HORIZON cardiovascular-outcomes trial (n=8,323): biomarker lowered as designed, but no reduction in CV death/MI/stroke/urgent revascularization vs placebo. This is the first-ever Phase 3 outcomes test of the Lp(a) hypothesis — and it failed. The counterargument (deeper suppression needed; olpasiran/leopdisiran are siRNAs with >93–97% lowering and different trial designs) is legitimate but unproven; the readouts arrive in 2028–2029. Until then, any Lp(a)-lowering program — including zerlasiran — carries target-class risk that no amount of biomarker data can remove. FACT HORIZON reports BioSpace INTERPRETATION
R-BBiomarker ≠ outcome, twice over. Zerlasiran has only ever shown Lp(a) lowering — never a cardiovascular event reduction. Divesiran has only ever shown phlebotomy avoidance and HCT control — never a reduction in thrombotic events, which is the actual morbidity driver in PV. Both programs' clinical value is assumed by surrogate. INTERPRETATION
R-CDivesiran's biology has a thalassemia-shaped caution flag. The same TMPRSS6-silencing approach was tested in non-transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia (GEMINI II, NCT04718844); a recent peer-reviewed hematology review notes that divesiran "has not progressed further, with no announced plans for continued development" in that indication after preliminary results did not show clear clinically meaningful benefit on hemoglobin or transfusion burden — the same pattern seen with Ionis's TMPRSS6 antisense sapablursen. Iron restriction can control red-cell quantity (PV) without fixing red-cell quality (thalassemia) — but it also means divesiran's benefit is confined to a narrow physiologic trick. FACT Am J Hematol review
R-DIron restriction is a double-edged sword. Pharmacologic TMPRSS6 suppression mimics a state (high hepcidin / low iron availability) that in germline form causes iron-refractory iron deficiency anemia. In PV the goal is to restrain overproduction — but overshoot risks anemia: the Phase 2 readout already included two grade-1 anemia events. Chronic iron restriction's long-term consequences in a PV population (fatigue, restless legs, cognition) are unknown beyond 36–60 weeks of observation. INTERPRETATION PV Reporter (anemia events)
R-EPlatform-class risks of siRNA. GalNAc-siRNA is now a validated modality for hepatic targets, but class concerns remain: off-target RISC loading, long pharmacodynamic tails that complicate safety management (a bad effect can't be quickly "washed out" when dosing is quarterly), and immunogenicity/injection-site burden. Silence's datasets are small (n=178 largest) and short; none is powered to detect rare safety signals. INTERPRETATION

4.1 SANRECO — divesiran Phase 2 in polycythemia vera (the key binary, already read out)

Design elementDetail
Design36-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2; 48 phlebotomy-dependent PV patients. FACT SANRECO PR
DosingDivesiran 6 mg/kg subcutaneous every 6 weeks (Q6W) or every 12 weeks (Q12W) vs placebo. FACT
Primary endpointProportion of patients achieving clinical response = absence of phlebotomy AND maintenance of hematocrit <45% during weeks 18–36. FACT
Key secondaryPhlebotomy rate during weeks 0–36; plus HCT control, iron markers (ferritin), and MPN-SAF Total Symptom Score. FACT
Result (primary)88% divesiran vs 19% placebo; p<0.0001. Placebo-adjusted response ≈ 69 percentage points. By regimen: 93.8% (Q6W) and 81.3% (Q12W). FACT
Sensitivity analysisIn the 40-patient subset meeting the stricter Phase 1 phlebotomy criterion (≥3 phlebotomies in the prior 3 months or ≥5 in the prior 12 months): 89% divesiran vs 8% placebo met the response definition (HCT <45% without phlebotomy, weeks 18–36), p<0.0001. Directionally consistent with the primary analysis — a subset, not a replacement for it. FACT 8-K summary
Result (secondary)Mean phlebotomies per patient weeks 0–36: 0.2 (divesiran) vs 2.1 (placebo); p<0.0001. Improvements reported in HCT control, ferritin, and MPN-SAF TSS (detailed data not yet released). FACT (detail-level data GAP)
Safety"Well tolerated with no new safety findings." Independent reporting: injection-site reactions infrequent and self-resolving; two grade-1 (mild) anemia events. FACT PV Reporter company PR
ExtensionAll 48 patients completed the placebo-controlled portion and are now in 3-year double-blind and open-label extension periods; company plans to present full results at an upcoming medical congress (abstract submitted to ASH). FACT company PR 8-K

Statistical/powering read (honest version): The company has not published the trial's formal power calculation or the pre-specified hierarchical testing sequence beyond the primary + key secondary GAP. What is visible is encouraging but small: n=48 total (≈16/arm), a 36-week double-blind window, and an endpoint (phlebotomy avoidance over weeks 18–36) that is clinically meaningful to patients but is not the hard thrombotic-event endpoint that ultimately defines PV therapy value. A 69-point placebo-adjusted delta is large enough to be robust to the small n — but small trials overestimate effect sizes, and the Q6W-vs-Q12W gap (93.8% vs 81.3%) hints at a dose-frequency response the Phase 3 (planned Q12W) must confirm. INTERPRETATION

Phase 1 context (EHA 2026, abstract PF886, n=21): 80 phlebotomies in the 6 months before treatment vs 5 during dosing and 4 in the 16-week follow-up; ferritin rose 28.1 → 48.0 μg/L at week 34 (P=.035) while serum iron/TSAT fell (iron redistribution, not removal); no treatment-related SAEs or discontinuations at doses up to 9 mg/kg; majority improved on MPN-10 symptom score. Open-label, uncontrolled — directional only. FACT OncLive / EHA 2026

Design elementDetail
DesignDouble-blind, placebo-controlled, 60-week Phase 2; 178 patients with Lp(a) ≥125 nmol/L (median baseline ~215 nmol/L) at high ASCVD risk. FACT MDedge
DosingZerlasiran 300 mg SC every 16 weeks, 300 mg every 24 weeks, or 450 mg every 24 weeks. FACT

10 / RISKS

Ranked red flags

Ordered by potential damage to the thesis, not by recency.

#1The Lp(a) hypothesis failed its first Phase 3 — zerlasiran's reason to exist is now a question mark. Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) ~72–80% and still missed its CV-outcomes primary (Sep 4, 2026). Zerlasiran lowers the same biomarker deeper, but no Lp(a) drug has ever reduced CV events. The asset is Phase 3-ready with no partner — and the partner search is happening in the worst possible news environment for the class. INTERPRETATION
#2Zerlasiran is stranded capital. Years of R&D and manufacturing scale-up sit idle while the company explicitly refuses to self-fund Phase 3. Every quarter without a partner is option value decaying — and a weak deal (low upfront, heavy rights give-up) may be worse than none for the stock. INTERPRETATION
#3CEO ousted; interim chair-led leadership for ~10 months. Craig Tooman departed by "mutual consent" Dec 14, 2025; Chairman Iain Ross is interim PEO with no permanent CEO named as of Oct 2026. Leadership vacuums at binary-stage biotechs correlate with strategic drift and weak negotiating posture on partnerships. FACT on the change; INTERPRETATION on the implication
#4Phase 3 PV trial needs funding the balance sheet doesn't have. ~$72M cash vs a Phase 3 that will cost multiples of that; guided start 1H 2027. The financing will likely come via dilution (16% option overhang already) or a divesiran partnership that caps upside. INTERPRETATION
#5SANRECO was small and surrogate-based. n=48, 36 weeks, endpoint = phlebotomy avoidance + HCT <45%. No thrombosis data, no clonal data, no long-term iron-restriction safety. Phase 3 could confirm — or could show the effect attenuates, anemia accumulates, or regulators demand harder endpoints. INTERPRETATION
#6Partners keep walking away. Hansoh terminated (Dec 2024); Mallinckrodt concluded; AstraZeneca declined to carry SLN312 past Phase 1. Three data points of pharma diligence ending in "no" is a pattern worth more than any single press release's optimism. INTERPRETATION
#7Thalassemia was the same drug failing on a related biology. Divesiran/SLN124 showed no clear clinically meaningful benefit in NTDT thalassemia and the program was quietly dropped — a reminder that TMPRSS6 iron restriction is not a general hematology fix. FACT Am J Hematol review

Bear case

  • Zerlasiran never finds a Phase 3 partner at acceptable economics; the asset is written down to option value near zero.
  • Divesiran Phase 3 (1H 2027) is delayed by funding; a dilutive raise lands with the stock weak, or the trial shows attenuated effect / anemia signal at scale.
  • Olpasiran's 2028 readout fails too, killing the Lp(a) class — zerlasiran becomes unpartnerable at any price.
  • Cash burn forces a raise below $9.82 with the 16% option overhang weighing every rally.
  • Net: a single-asset PV company with a funded-or-not Phase 3 and a stranded CV asset. Downside is balance-sheet and dilution arithmetic.

Base case

  • Divesiran Phase 3 initiates on schedule (1H 2027) with disclosed design; funding via a measured raise and/or a regional divesiran partnership.
  • Zerlasiran partner search continues; a deal eventually arrives with modest upfront + milestones — value is back-end loaded and shared.
  • SLN098/SLN365 reach IND in 2027, keeping the platform narrative alive; SLN548 finds a partner or is shelved.
  • Permanent CEO named; governance discount fades.
  • Net: value concentrates on divesiran's PV binary; zerlasiran is a free-ish call option on the Lp(a) hypothesis being rescued by Amgen/Lilly data in 2028–29.

Bull case

  • SANRECO effect reproduces in Phase 3 on Q12W dosing with clean long-term safety; divesiran becomes the first siRNA and the least-frequently-dosed option in PV — a best-in-class profile in a chronic disease with high treatment burden.
  • Zerlasiran lands a well-structured partnership (meaningful upfront, partner-funded CVOT) — or Amgen's OCEAN(a) (2028) rehabilitates the Lp(a) hypothesis and re-rates the asset.
  • Platform validation: two independent clinical-stage siRNAs (TMPRSS6, LPA) + AZ-validated ANGPTL3 Phase 1 data attract a broader platform bid. Any takeout/max-value scenario is capped at published sell-side targets (max cited: $75).
