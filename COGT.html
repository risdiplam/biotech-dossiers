03 / SCIENCE

One mutation, three diseases — and a selectivity argument that is measurable

KIT D816V is a constitutively active kinase mutation in exon 17 that drives ~95% of systemic mastocytosis and appears as a secondary resistance mutation in GIST. Bezuclastinib is a type I inhibitor built to occupy the active conformation of mutant KIT while sparing the closely related kinases whose inhibition produces the class's dose-limiting toxicities — and to stay out of the brain.

FACT Plain-language version: mast cells and many GIST tumors run on a stuck "on" switch called KIT. In most mastocytosis patients the switch is broken by a single-letter change, D816V, that imatinib-class drugs cannot touch because the mutation changes the shape they bind to. Bezuclastinib was designed against that exact shape. The engineering claim is precision: hit the mutant switch, leave its closest molecular cousins (wild-type KIT, PDGFRα/β, CSF1R, VEGFR2) alone, because hitting those cousins is what causes the swelling, effusions, bleeding and cognitive problems that limit the older drugs — and keep the drug out of the brain, where the incumbent, avapritinib, carries warnings for intracranial hemorrhage and cognitive effects.

The selectivity data (cellular assays, head-to-head)

FACT In Cogent's comparative cellular profiling (AACR-NCI-EORTC 2021; company poster, corroborated in the peer-reviewed APEX methods literature): bezuclastinib inhibited KIT V560G/D816V phosphorylation at IC50 14 nM, while phospho-PDGFRα, phospho-PDGFRβ and phospho-CSF1R all remained >10,000 nM — windows of >700-fold. In the same assays avapritinib read 13 nM (KIT), 53 nM (PDGFRα), 10 nM (PDGFRβ) and 249 nM (CSF1R): i.e., avapritinib inhibits PDGFRβ at least as potently as its target. In Ba/F3 growth assays, bezuclastinib IC50 was 6.6 nM against KIT D816V and 7.1 nM against V560G/D816V, versus 61 nM against wild-type KIT (Gebreyohannes et al., and vendor data summaries of the same work). Brain penetration in rat tissue distribution was minimal (brain:plasma ratio <0.1 at clinically correlated exposures).

INTERPRETATION Two honest qualifications. First, "wild-type sparing" is relative, not absolute: the wild-type KIT window in growth assays is ~9-fold, and the drug plainly does inhibit wild-type KIT at clinical exposure — the clinical correlates (hair depigmentation in 31–70% of patients across programs) are on-target KIT pharmacology, not an accident. Second, the clinical record so far is consistent with the design intent where it matters most: no intracranial bleeding and no treatment-related cognitive impairment signal emerged in APEX Part 1 or the pivotal programs, and edema rates, while present (periorbital/peripheral ~13% in APEX Part 1), sit well below the avapritinib experience. The selectivity claim is therefore best read as a toxicity-shaping claim, not an efficacy claim — efficacy against D816V is comparable in kind to avapritinib; the differentiation is what patients don't have to tolerate.

How it could fail scientifically

INTERPRETATION (1) The transaminase signal is real, reproducible across all three programs, and mechanistically unexplained in public data — if FDA treats it as a class-level hepatic liability, the label could carry monitoring that erases the convenience advantage. (2) In GIST the drug is not used alone: the PEAK regimen bolts bezuclastinib onto sunitinib, itself a multi-kinase inhibitor with its own toxicity, so the "clean selective drug" story is diluted in the largest-revenue indication. (3) Resistance: type I KIT inhibitors remain vulnerable to activation-loop and gatekeeper evolution; durability beyond the PEAK/OS follow-up is unproven, and overall survival in PEAK is still immature.

Key literature: DeAngelo et al., APEX design/methods, HemaSphere (EHA abstract book, PMC9431291); Rein et al., SUMMIT primary analysis, Blood 2025;146(Suppl 1):80; Wagner et al., JAMA Oncology 2021 (Phase 1 bezuclastinib ± sunitinib in GIST); Gebreyohannes et al., Clin Exp Med 2019 (PDX); Gotlib et al. and the mIWG-MRT-ECNM response-criteria literature for AdvSM endpoints.

04 / CLINICAL

Three pivotal datasets, all positive — with one recurring safety footnote

Every number below is from the registrational datasets as presented at ASH 2025 (SUMMIT), EHA 2026 (APEX) and ASCO 2026 (PEAK), via company releases furnished on Form 8-K and congress reporting. Cross-trial comparisons to avapritinib are flagged as such and are not head-to-head evidence.

StudyDesignKey results
SUMMIT — NonAdvSM (NCT05186753)Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 (registration-directed Part 2): bezuclastinib 100 mg QD + best supportive care, n=119 randomized, vs placebo + BSC, n=60; primary endpoint mean change in total symptom score (TSS, MS2D2 diary) at week 24. Registry total enrollment 237 across Parts 1–3.Primary hit: mean TSS change at wk 24 −24.3 (−43%) vs −15.4 (−29%); difference −8.91 (95% CI −13.56 to −4.26), p<0.001 (topline p=0.0002). ≥30% TSS reduction 65.4% vs 38.6% (p<0.001); ≥50% reduction 34.3% vs 18.1% (p=0.01). Objective markers: ≥50% serum tryptase reduction 87.4% (95.4% of evaluable at ASH) vs 0%; ≥50% bone-marrow mast-cell reduction or aggregate clearance 88.2% vs 25.5%; ≥50% KIT D816V VAF reduction 97.5% vs 0% (all p<0.0001). PEAK — 2L GIST (Phase 3)Randomized Phase 3, >440 enrolled: bezuclastinib + sunitinib 37.5 mg vs sunitinib 37.5 mg alone, in imatinib-resistant or -intolerant GIST; primary endpoint PFS by blinded independent central review. Cutoff Sep 30, 2025; presented ASCO 2026.Primary hit decisively: mPFS 16.5 vs 9.2 months, HR 0.50 (95% CI 0.39–0.65), p<0.0001 — a 50% reduction in risk of progression or death against an active comparator, the first positive randomized trial in second-line GIST in over 20 years (company characterization). ORR 46% vs 26% (p<0.0001). Overall survival immature. Mean treatment duration on the combination estimated 21.4 months among ongoing patients (Mar 31, 2026). An exon 9 subgroup showed 25.1-month mPFS, now the subject of a 40-patient first-line extension cohort.

Safety, program by program — the transaminase thread

FACT SUMMIT (24-week double-blind; bezu n=118 safety set): TEAEs 98.3% vs 88.3% placebo; ~70% of TEAEs grade 1; serious TEAEs 4.2% vs 5.0%. Most common: hair color changes 69.5% vs 5.0%, altered taste 23.7% vs 0%, nausea 22.0% vs 13.3%, ALT/AST elevation 22.0% vs 6.6%, headache 17.8%, alopecia 11.9%, ALP elevation 10.2%. Grade ≥3 ALT/AST occurred in 5.9%; dose reductions for TRAEs 11.0%; discontinuations for TRAEs 5.9% — every one attributable to transaminase elevations, all transient and fully resolved, none requiring hospitalization. Through 48 weeks, TEAE discontinuations were 9.2% (11/119).

FACT APEX (150 mg): most frequent TRAEs — hair color change ~31%, neutropenia ~31%, altered taste ~28%, thrombocytopenia ~25%, ALT/AST elevations ~21%. Dose reductions 14.8%; in the Dec 2025 topline cut there were no discontinuations due to TRAEs; at EHA, of two patients with grade 3 transaminase elevation, one discontinued and one continued after dose reduction. Transaminase elevations were predominantly low-grade, asymptomatic and reversible. In Part 1 (all doses, sicker and higher-dose cohorts): TRAEs 91%, grade ≥3 TRAEs 44%, dose reductions 34%, TRAE discontinuations 6% — including one grade 3 drug-induced liver injury — with no treatment-related cognitive impairment, no intracranial bleeding and no treatment-related deaths.

FACT PEAK (combination): the company characterizes the combination as well tolerated with no unique risks versus sunitinib's known profile. The specific asymmetry in the topline data: AE discontinuations 7.4% (combination) vs 3.8% (sunitinib alone), and grade 3+ liver-enzyme elevations led to dose reductions in 12.7% vs 1.5%. No life-threatening liver-enzyme abnormalities were reported.

INTERPRETATION Read together: bezuclastinib's tolerability advantage over avapritinib is genuine on the CNS/bleeding axis (the events that drive avapritinib's warnings are absent) and on edema, but it is not a clean drug — it trades those liabilities for a hepatic enzyme signal plus cosmetic hair depigmentation in a third to two-thirds of patients, and myelosuppression in advanced disease. Whether that trade is commercially decisive depends on the label language FDA attaches to the liver signal, which is why §05 is the section that matters most in this dossier.

06 / LANDSCAPE

The incumbent is real, growing, and owned by Sanofi

Bezuclastinib does not enter empty markets. Avapritinib (Ayvakit/Ayvakyt) is approved in AdvSM, indolent SM and PDGFRA-mutant GIST, and its franchise is compounding at roughly 50% a year. The clinical differentiation case — not the marketing case — rests on specific, checkable tolerability and efficacy contrasts.

Ayvakit (avapritinib) — the comp that matters

FACT Net product revenue $479M in 2024; ~$150M in Q1 2025 alone; Blueprint guided FY2025 to $700–720M and Sanofi (which acquired Blueprint for $9.1B upfront in 2025) reported pro-forma FY2025 Ayvakit sales of $725M, with Q1 2026 sales of €177M — a trajectory toward blockbuster status in 2026, and a $2B-by-2030 ambition stated by Blueprint. Avapritinib is the same target (KIT D816V) with a dirtier kinase profile: its label carries warnings for intracranial hemorrhage and cognitive effects, and edema is a signature toxicity.

Where bezuclastinib is clinically differentiated

FACT (1) No intracranial bleeding or treatment-related cognitive signal in the bezuclastinib pivotal programs to date, versus label warnings for avapritinib. (2) Cellular selectivity >700× over PDGFRβ/CSF1R versus ~1× for avapritinib (§03). (3) In AdvSM, APEX ORR 65% (mIWG) with 12-mo OS 87% compares favorably on a cross-trial basis to avapritinib's EXPLORER/PATHFINDER experience — cross-trial, different eras and adjudications; not a superiority claim. (4) In GIST, the PEAK combination beat an active standard head-to-head (HR 0.50), a claim no KIT inhibitor in 2L GIST has previously made.

Where it is not differentiated

FACT Midostaurin (Rydapt, approved AdvSM 2017) is a cheap, dirty multikinase fallback. In GIST, sunitinib is generic-era and the entrenched 2L standard; ripretinib (Qinlock) and regorafenib occupy later lines; and Sanofi retains next-generation SM assets from Blueprint (elenestinib; the wild-type KIT inhibitor BLU-808) that could answer bezuclastinib's tolerability pitch inside the decade. Bezuclastinib also shares the class's cosmetic signature (hair depigmentation) and adds its own hepatic one.

Commercial asymmetry

INTERPRETATION Cogent must out-execute a Sanofi sales machine with a first-time commercial organization across three launches in seven months, selling a tolerability story to the same concentrated prescriber base that already knows Ayvakit. The realistic base case is share-taking in new starts and switch patients (Cogent is already running an avapritinib-switch extension cohort in SUMMIT, results expected by end of 2026), not displacement of stable Ayvakit patients.

12 / RISKS

Ranked red flags

Ranked by expected value impact over the next twelve months, not by drama.

  1. Label risk on the liver signal (highest). Grade ≥3 transaminase elevations in ~6% (SUMMIT), grade 3+ hepatic dose reductions in 12.7% of the PEAK combination arm, one grade 3 DILI in APEX Part 1. FDA's rendering — monitoring schedule, warning placement, possible REMS-adjacent language — determines whether the tolerability thesis survives contact with the label, in all three indications simultaneously.
  2. NonAdvSM endpoint precedent. A chronic symptom-score (PRO) approval basis in a non-fatal population is the least precedented of the three files. A CRL here is the single most probable bear trigger and would likely drag the AdvSM file's perceived odds with it.
  3. Priced for the sweep. At ~$4.8B EV with ~16% cash-to-price, the equity embeds substantial approval and launch success. Asymmetric payoff now runs the other way versus the library's pre-data names: clean approvals confirm; any single failure de-rates the whole molecule.
  4. First-time commercial execution vs Sanofi. Three launches in seven months against an entrenched incumbent (~$725M and growing ~50%/yr) with a global rare-disease machine and next-generation SM assets (elenestinib, BLU-808) behind it.
  5. GIST is a combination, not a clean label. The 2L GIST product is bezuclastinib plus generic sunitinib: combination tolerability (AE discontinuations 7.4% vs 3.8%), co-promotion complexity, and an immature OS dataset that payers and guidelines will eventually demand.
  6. Continuous ATM dilution. A fresh $400M facility, a demonstrated pattern of pre-catalyst selling, and 32.1M equity awards outstanding (~17% of the as-converted count) at a $9.54 blended WAEP on the main pool — a structural transfer from shareholders to employees and new buyers that continues regardless of outcomes.
  7. Converts overhang at $44.95. The $230M 1.625% notes due 2031 convert into ~5.12M shares just above the base-case range — success itself reintroduces dilution and caps the bull arithmetic.
  8. Single-molecule company. All three NDAs are the same drug: a CMC, safety or supply event (note the single-source Hovione spray-dried-dispersion/tablet agreement) touches every indication at once. The pipeline behind it (JAK2, KRAS) is pre-IND and cannot backfill a bezuclastinib failure.
